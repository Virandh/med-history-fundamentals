# Psychiatric Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

The psychiatric screen samples mood, thought, perception and risk. Remember it as a descent from the common to the critical, always ending on risk:

> **MOOD → ANXIETY → PSYCHOSIS → COGNITION → SUBSTANCES → RISK**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Mood** | Depression / mania | Low mood, anhedonia, sleep, appetite, energy, guilt; elevated mood/overactivity |
| **Anxiety** | Anxiety spectrum | Worry, panic, phobias, obsessions/compulsions |
| **Psychosis** | Thought & perception | Hallucinations, delusions, thought interference |
| **Cognition** | Orientation/memory | Confusion, memory (overlaps neuro) |
| **Substances** | Alcohol & drugs | Use, dependence, withdrawal |
| **Risk** | Safety | Self-harm, suicide, risk to others, self-neglect |

**Risk is never optional and always last-but-not-least.** The golden rule: asking about suicide does not plant the idea — it is a required, protective part of the screen. Two mnemonics do the heavy lifting: **SIGECAPS** for depression and **CAGE** for alcohol.

## The questions

### 1. Mood — depression — *"How has your mood been? Do you still enjoy the things you used to?"*

**Why we ask:** Low mood plus anhedonia are the two core features; the SIGECAPS cluster establishes whether threshold criteria are met and gauges severity.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"In the past month, have you felt down, depressed or hopeless — and have you lost interest or pleasure in things?"* — The two-question screen (either positive) has a good negative likelihood ratio — a 'no' to both makes major depression unlikely
- *"How has your sleep and appetite been, and has your weight changed?"* — Two of the SIGECAPS somatic markers — disturbed in either direction (too much or too little) counts
- *"How's your energy and concentration been?"* — Two more SIGECAPS domains — ask concretely (reading a page, following a TV show) rather than accepting a vague 'fine'
- *"Have you been feeling guilty, worthless, or that you're a burden?"* — Guilt/worthlessness is a core cognitive symptom and, combined with hopelessness, raises suicide risk specifically
- *"Has anyone noticed you've slowed down, or that you seem restless and can't settle?"* — Psychomotor retardation or agitation, observed by others, adds objective weight to the history
- *"How long has this been going on — is it more than two weeks?"* — Duration threshold matters for the diagnosis, and distinguishes an episode from a brief reactive dip
- *"Any physical illness, thyroid problems, or new medications that started around the same time?"* — Screens the organic mimics — hypothyroidism, Parkinson's, chronic illness, interferons/chemotherapy — before assuming primary depression
- **!** *"Sometimes when people feel this low, they have thoughts that life isn't worth living — have you had thoughts like that?"* — The bridge into the dedicated suicide-risk screen — never skip it because the mood questions felt complete without it

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Low mood or anhedonia (at least one of the two core features required) + a total of ≥5 SIGECAPS symptoms for ≥2 weeks** → major depression
- Screen for organic mimics: hypothyroidism, Parkinson's disease, chronic illness (e.g. HIV, heart failure), and drugs (interferons, chemotherapy) — depression still deserves a physical differential (the beta-blocker–depression link is contested; anaemia mimics the fatigue presentation rather than depression itself)
- **Low mood + chronic pain/illness** → reactive/adjustment vs comorbid depression
- SIGECAPS = Sleep, Interest (anhedonia), Guilt/worthlessness, Energy, Concentration, Appetite/weight, Psychomotor change, Suicidality

### 2. Mood — mania — *"Any times you felt unusually high, energetic, needing little sleep, doing risky things?"*

**Why we ask:** You cannot diagnose depression safely without screening for bipolarity — antidepressants can precipitate mania, and a missed manic history changes management entirely.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Have there been times you felt unusually good about yourself, or on top of the world?"* — Elevated or expansive mood is the core affective feature
- *"Any times you needed much less sleep than usual but didn't feel tired?"* — Reduced need for sleep (not insomnia — the patient feels rested on less) is one of the more specific manic features
- *"Did you feel especially talkative, or find your thoughts racing?"* — Pressured speech and flight of ideas are classic accompanying features
- *"Any spending sprees, risky decisions, or behaviour out of character?"* — Disinhibited, risky behaviour (spending, sexual indiscretion) is often what brings the episode to someone else's attention, not the patient's own
- *"Did you feel especially important, or that you had special powers or abilities?"* — Grandiosity, when extreme, can shade into delusional — worth asking directly
- *"How long did this last, and did it disrupt your normal life?"* — A week or more, disruptive → mania (bipolar I); 4+ days, less severe → hypomania (bipolar II)
- *"Has anyone else noticed this and been concerned?"* — Collateral history matters — patients in a manic episode often lack insight and see nothing wrong
- *"Could this be related to stimulant use, or a thyroid problem?"* — Distinguish a true manic episode from the sustained overactivity of stimulant intoxication or hyperthyroidism

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Elevated/irritable mood + reduced need for sleep + grandiosity + overspending/risk-taking + pressured speech, sustained days** → manic episode (bipolar)
- Distinguish from the sustained overactivity of stimulant use or hyperthyroidism

### 3. Generalised worry — *"Do you worry a lot, more days than not?"*

**Why we ask:** Persistent, hard-to-control worry across multiple areas of life, with physical tension, is generalised anxiety disorder — common and frequently somatised.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is the worry hard to control, and does it spread across different areas of your life, not just one thing?"* — Diffuse, hard-to-control worry across domains, not focused on one specific fear, is what separates GAD from a phobia or a situational worry
- *"Has this been most days for six months or more?"* — Chronicity is part of the diagnostic threshold, not just intensity
- *"Any physical tension — restlessness, poor sleep, muscle tension, feeling on edge?"* — The physical tension cluster is what patients often present with instead of naming the worry itself

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Persistent excessive worry most days, with restlessness/tension/poor sleep** → generalised anxiety

---

### 4. Panic attacks — *"Do you get sudden attacks of intense fear with physical symptoms, out of the blue?"*

**Why we ask:** Panic disorder is a real and common diagnosis, but it's also a diagnosis of exclusion in the moment — the physical symptoms overlap completely with cardiac, respiratory and endocrine emergencies.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How quickly does it build up, and how long does it last?"* — Builds rapidly, peaks within minutes, and settles — the sudden crescendo-then-resolve pattern is characteristic of panic rather than a sustained medical process
- *"What goes through your mind during it — fear of dying, losing control, or going mad?"* — The catastrophic cognitions (fear of a heart attack, losing control) are part of the diagnostic picture, not just the physical symptoms
- *"Does it happen out of the blue, in situations where most people wouldn't be afraid?"* — Unprovoked, situationally inappropriate attacks distinguish panic disorder from a fear response to genuine danger

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Discrete attacks of terror with palpitations, chest tightness, paraesthesiae, fear of dying** → panic disorder — but exclude hyperthyroidism, phaeochromocytoma, arrhythmia/MI and PE first

---

### 5. Obsessions & compulsions — *"Any repetitive thoughts that keep intruding, or rituals you feel you have to do?"*

**Why we ask:** OCD is defined by the pairing — intrusive thoughts causing distress, and compulsions performed to relieve that distress — not by either alone.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Do you have thoughts that keep intruding even though you try to stop them?"* — Obsessions are ego-dystonic — the patient recognises them as excessive or senseless, unlike a delusion
- *"Do you feel you have to do something — checking, washing, counting — to make the anxiety go away?"* — Compulsions performed specifically to neutralise the obsession's distress complete the OCD pairing
- *"How much time does this take up each day, and does it get in the way of things?"* — Time cost and functional interference are what separate OCD from mild everyday habits or superstitions

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Intrusive thoughts + compulsive rituals to relieve them** → OCD

---

### 6. Trauma & PTSD — *"Has anything very frightening or traumatic happened to you? Do you get flashbacks or avoid things that remind you of it?"*

**Why we ask:** PTSD requires a specific link to trauma exposure, distinguishing it from anxiety that arises without a triggering event.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Do you get unwanted memories, nightmares, or flashbacks of what happened?"* — Intrusive re-experiencing is the core PTSD feature, distinguishing it from anxiety without a specific trigger
- *"Do you avoid places, people or situations that remind you of it?"* — Avoidance behaviour is a required diagnostic feature, not just distress about the memory
- *"Are you more jumpy, on edge, or sleeping poorly since it happened?"* — Hyperarousal completes the diagnostic picture, and timing matters — under a month favours acute stress disorder, over a month favours PTSD

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Anxiety tied to a specific trauma with flashbacks/avoidance/hypervigilance** → PTSD

### 7. Psychosis — *"Ever heard or seen things others couldn't? Held beliefs others found unusual, or felt your thoughts interfered with?"*

**Why we ask:** Screens perception and thought. Ask matter-of-factly. First-rank-type symptoms and their tempo help separate primary psychosis, mood-congruent psychosis, and organic/drug-induced causes.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Have you ever heard voices or sounds when no one else was there?"* — Establishes true auditory hallucination — ask matter-of-factly, without judgement, to get an honest answer
- *"Do the voices talk about you to each other, or comment on what you're doing?"* — Third-person or running-commentary voices are more specific for schizophrenia than a single voice
- *"Do you ever feel your thoughts are being interfered with — put into your head, taken out, or broadcast so others can hear them?"* — Thought interference (insertion, withdrawal, broadcast) is a classic first-rank symptom of schizophrenia
- *"Do you have any beliefs that other people find hard to accept, or feel that people are watching, following, or plotting against you?"* — Screens for delusions — persecutory themes are the most common
- *"How long has this been going on, and did it come on suddenly or build up gradually?"* — Chronic, gradual onset supports a primary psychotic illness; acute onset with clouded consciousness points to delirium or a drug/organic cause instead
- *"Does this fit with a time your mood was very high or very low?"* — Psychosis congruent with extreme mood (grandiose in mania, nihilistic in severe depression) points to a mood disorder with psychotic features rather than schizophrenia
- **!** *"Any recent drug or alcohol use, or a physical illness or fever?"* — Substance-induced and organic/delirium-related psychosis need to be excluded before assuming a primary psychiatric cause
- *"How is this affecting your daily life — work, relationships, looking after yourself?"* — Functional impact and duration (six months) are part of what distinguishes a full psychotic disorder from a brief or attenuated episode

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Auditory hallucinations (esp. third-person/running commentary) + delusions + thought interference, chronic** → schizophrenia spectrum
- **Psychosis congruent with extreme mood (grandiose in mania, nihilistic in severe depression)** → mood disorder with psychotic features
- **Acute onset with clouded consciousness/fluctuation, visual hallucinations** → think delirium/organic or drug-induced — a medical, not primary psychiatric, emergency

### 8. Cognition — *"Any problems with memory or concentration?"*

**Why we ask:** Bridges to neurology; distinguishes depression ("pseudodementia"), delirium and dementia — which have very different urgencies. (See the neuro delirium/dementia entry.)

**If yes, ask:**
- *"What day is it, and do you know where you are right now?"* — A quick orientation check — errors here point towards delirium or dementia rather than a purely mood-related complaint
- *"Did this come on suddenly, over days, or has it crept up gradually over months to years?"* — Sudden/fluctuating → delirium; gradual and progressive → dementia; a discrete depressive episode → 'pseudodementia', which improves as mood improves
- *"Does your concentration or memory vary through the day, or is it consistently the same?"* — Fluctuation, especially worse at night, favours delirium; a steady deficit favours dementia
- *"Has anyone else noticed this, and is it affecting your daily activities — finances, medications, safety?"* — Collateral history and functional impact matter more than the patient's own insight, which can be reduced in both dementia and psychosis

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Sudden, fluctuating confusion, worse at night** → delirium — find the systemic cause
- **Gradual, progressive memory/orientation loss over months to years, consciousness clear** → dementia
- **Reports "don't know" with poor effort, in the context of low mood** → depressive pseudodementia, which improves as mood improves

### 9. Substances — *"How much alcohol in a typical week? Any recreational drugs?"*

**Why we ask:** Substance use causes, mimics and complicates every other psychiatric presentation, and withdrawal can be life-threatening. Quantify, and screen for dependence.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How much alcohol do you drink in a typical week, and how many days do you drink?"* — Quantify in standard drinks — patients under-report, so ask specifically rather than accepting 'socially'
- *"Have you ever felt you should cut down, or felt annoyed by people criticising your drinking? Felt guilty, or needed a drink first thing in the morning?"* — CAGE — two or more positive answers is a strong screen for dependence
- **!** *"Any shakes, sweats, or seizures if you go a while without a drink?"* — Physical withdrawal symptoms confirm dependence and flag a withdrawal that can itself be life-threatening
- *"Any recreational or illicit drug use — what, how often, and how do you take it?"* — Route of use (injecting) and the specific substance both change the risk profile and screening needed
- *"Any use of stimulants — cocaine, amphetamines?"* — Can mimic or precipitate mania, psychosis, or panic — important to distinguish from a primary psychiatric episode
- *"Any opioid use, prescribed or otherwise?"* — Carries specific withdrawal and overdose risks that need their own safety-netting
- *"Has your drinking or drug use affected your relationships, work, health or finances?"* — Screens the complications — social, hepatic, GI bleeding, nutritional — that follow from dependence
- *"Have you ever tried to cut down or stop, and what happened?"* — Prior quit attempts and their outcome, including withdrawal severity, inform how this attempt should be managed

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Positive CAGE (Cut down, Annoyed, Guilty, Eye-opener; ≥2 positive) / high intake + morning drinking + withdrawal (tremor, sweats, seizures)** → alcohol dependence (current Australian first-line screening uses AUDIT-C; CAGE is the older, dependence-weighted tool)
- Screen for complications: mood, liver, GI bleeding, nutrition, social
- Ask about stimulants (can mimic mania/psychosis/panic) and opioids (withdrawal, overdose risk)

### 10. Self-harm & suicide risk — *"Sometimes when people feel this low they have thoughts life isn't worth living, or of harming themselves. Have you had thoughts like that?"*

**Why we ask:** The single most important part of the psychiatric review. Escalate gently through the stages; a plan, means, and intent mark high risk.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Have you had thoughts that life isn't worth living, or of ending your life?"* — The direct question — asking does not plant the idea, and many patients feel relief at being asked
- *"How often do these thoughts come, and how does it feel when they do?"* — Frequency and how distressing/intrusive they are helps gauge how close to acting the thoughts sit
- **!** *"Have you thought about how you might do it?"* — Moving from ideation to a specific method is a significant step up in risk
- **!** *"Have you made any actual plans, or taken any steps — like saving up tablets or writing a note?"* — A concrete plan, especially with preparatory steps taken, marks a much higher acute risk than ideation alone
- **!** *"Do you have access to the means — medications, a weapon, or anything else you were thinking of using?"* — Access to means is one of the strongest modifiable risk factors — restricting access is itself a safety intervention
- **!** *"Have you ever acted on these feelings or attempted to harm yourself before?"* — A past attempt is one of the strongest predictors of a future one — always ask specifically, not just about current ideation
- *"What's stopping you — family, beliefs, pets, future plans?"* — Protective factors matter for the overall risk formulation, but never let their presence override a positive plan, means and intent
- *"Is there anyone at home, or is anyone aware of how you're feeling right now?"* — Establishes the immediate safety net and whether the patient is truly alone with these thoughts

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Active ideation + plan + means + intent + prior attempts** → high acute risk — needs urgent safety action, never send away
- **Fleeting passive thoughts, no plan/intent, strong protective factors and support** → lower acute risk, but document and safety-net

**Red flag:** Never send away a patient with a plan, means and intent — act on safety.

---

### 11. Risk to others & self-neglect — *"Any thoughts of harming anyone else? How are you managing day to day — eating, washing, paying bills?"*

**Why we ask:** Risk assessment isn't only about the patient's own safety — thoughts of harming others and an inability to care for oneself both change the urgency and the disposition.

**If yes, ask:**
- **!** *"Have you had thoughts of harming anyone else, or a specific person in mind?"* — A named, specific target changes this from a general question into a duty-to-warn situation needing urgent escalation
- *"How are you managing day to day — eating regularly, washing, taking your medications, paying bills?"* — Self-neglect can be a silent, high-vulnerability finding even without any active risk of harm
- *"Is your home safe, and do you have people checking in on you?"* — Living conditions and social support shape the disposition decision as much as the mental state itself

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Specific threats or plans toward a named person** → duty-to-warn considerations, urgent escalation
- **Self-neglect (not eating, not managing medications/finances, unsafe living conditions)** → vulnerability requiring support, even without active risk

## Discriminator table: delirium vs dementia vs depression

| Clue | Delirium | Dementia | Depression |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onset | Acute (hours–days) | Insidious (months–years) | Weeks |
| Course | Fluctuating, worse at night | Slowly progressive | Diurnal (worse mornings) |
| Consciousness | Clouded / altered | Clear until late | Clear |
| Attention | Impaired | Normal early | Variable |
| Answers | Muddled | Confabulates / near-misses | “Don’t know” (poor effort) |
| Reversible? | Yes — find the cause | Usually not | Yes — treat |

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

MOOD: depression (low mood + anhedonia + SIGECAPS), mania screen · ANXIETY: worry, panic, phobias, OCD, PTSD · PSYCHOSIS: hallucinations, delusions, thought interference · COGNITION: memory/concentration (delirium vs dementia vs depression) · SUBSTANCES: alcohol (CAGE + quantity), drugs · RISK: self-harm/suicide (ideation→plan→means→intent→past attempts), risk to others, self-neglect
