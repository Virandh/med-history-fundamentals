# Reproductive & Sexual History Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

The reproductive history has one shared trunk and two sex-specific branches. Take the general sexual history first (it applies to everyone and sets the risk context), then branch:

> **GENERAL → FEMALE → MALE**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **General** | Sexual activity & risk | Activity, partners, contraception, pain, fertility, STI risk |
| **Female** | Menstrual, pelvic, endocrine, screening | LMP, cycle, bleeding, discharge, pregnancy, screening |
| **Male** | Genital, functional, screening | Discharge, testicular, erectile function, prostate |

**Framing matters here more than anywhere else.** Signpost why you're asking ("I ask everyone these questions, to make sure I don't miss anything"), use inclusive language, don't assume orientation or partners, and confirm confidentiality. The clinical red flags — ectopic pregnancy, testicular torsion, postmenopausal bleeding — sit inside an intensely personal history, so the manner of asking is part of the skill.

---

## The questions

### 1. General sexual history — *"I’d like to ask some questions about your sexual health — I ask everyone these. Are you sexually active at the moment?"*

**Why we ask:** Frames every reproductive symptom, sets STI and blood-borne-virus risk, and is essential before interpreting abdominal or pelvic pain. Ask sensitively, without assumptions.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Discharge + dysuria + new partner** → STI (chlamydia, gonorrhoea)
- Genital ulcer — painful (herpes) vs painless (syphilis chancre)
- **Deep dyspareunia + pelvic pain** → endometriosis, PID
- **BBV risk factors** → offer testing (HIV, hepatitis B/C)

---

### 2. Female — menstrual history — *"When was your last period? Are they regular? Any bleeding between periods, after sex, or after menopause?"*

**Why we ask:** The menstrual pattern is a vital sign of reproductive health, and the possibility of pregnancy reframes any abdominal pain.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Postmenopausal bleeding** → endometrial cancer until excluded
- **Postcoital bleeding** → cervical pathology (ectropion, cervicitis, cervical cancer) — check screening history
- **Intermenstrual bleeding** → cervical or endometrial pathology — an indication for investigation (hysteroscopy ± endometrial biopsy)
- **Heavy regular periods** → common cause of iron-deficiency anaemia
- **Secondary amenorrhoea** → pregnancy first, then endocrine (thyroid, prolactin, PCOS)

**Red flag:** Postmenopausal bleeding is endometrial cancer until excluded.

---

### 3. Female — pelvic & pregnancy — *"Any pelvic pain or vaginal discharge? Is there any chance you could be pregnant?"*

**Why we ask:** The possibility of pregnancy reframes any abdominal pain, and discharge patterns separate infective causes.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Abdominal pain + missed period + possible pregnancy** → ectopic pregnancy until excluded (a can't-miss)
- **Discharge + pain + fever** → PID; itchy discharge → candida (bacterial vaginosis is characteristically non-itchy — its clue is a thin grey, fishy-smelling discharge, not pruritus)
- Always establish pregnancy status before imaging or prescribing

**Red flag:** Abdominal pain + missed period → ectopic pregnancy until excluded.

---

### 4. Female — endocrine & screening — *"Any milk discharge from the breasts, excess hair or acne, or menopausal symptoms? Are you up to date with cervical and breast screening?"*

**Why we ask:** Links reproductive symptoms to the endocrine axes and captures the two cancer-screening programs.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Nipple discharge / galactorrhoea** → prolactin (cross-reference pituitary)
- **Hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, acne)** → PCOS
- **Menopausal symptoms (hot flushes, mood swings, night sweats)** → menopause

---

### 5. Male — genital & testicular — *"Any discharge from the penis, rashes or ulcers? Any testicular pain, lumps or swelling?"*

**Why we ask:** Screens the STI causes and the two testicular red flags.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Urethral discharge** → STI screen
- **Painless testicular lump** → testicular cancer until excluded
- **Painful swelling + dysuria/discharge** → epididymo-orchitis

**Red flag:** Sudden severe testicular pain in a young man → testicular torsion (surgical emergency).

---

### 6. Male — function & screening — *"Any difficulty with erections? Any urinary symptoms or discussion about prostate checks?"*

**Why we ask:** Erectile dysfunction is an early vascular marker (shares risk factors with IHD) and a common drug/endocrine effect; prostate links to the GU voiding screen.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **ED + cardiovascular risk factors** → endothelial/vascular disease; review antihypertensives, antidepressants, and mood
- Prostate: voiding LUTS (cross-reference GU), family history, screening discussion

---

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

**GENERAL:** active?, partners (gender/number/type), contraception/condoms, dyspareunia, fertility, STI contact, BBV risk (IVDU/tattoos), genital lesions · **FEMALE:** LMP + cycle + volume, dysmenorrhoea, abnormal bleeding (postmenopausal/postcoital/intermenstrual), pelvic pain + discharge, pregnancy status + obstetric history, galactorrhoea/hyperandrogenism/menopause, cervical + breast screening · **MALE:** urethral discharge, testicular pain/lump, erectile function, prostate screening
