# Endocrine Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

The endocrine screen is best remembered gland-by-gland, because each gland's excess/deficiency has a signature. Run down the axis:

> **THYROID → GLUCOSE → ADRENAL → PITUITARY → CALCIUM/GONADAL**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Thyroid** | Thyroid | Heat/cold intolerance, weight, bowels, tremor, neck swelling, palpitations |
| **Glucose** | Pancreatic islets | Hyper- and hypoglycaemia symptoms, complications |
| **Adrenal** | Adrenal cortex | Fatigue/pigmentation (Addison); central weight, striae (Cushing) |
| **Pituitary** | Pituitary | Headache/visual fields, acral growth, galactorrhoea |
| **Calcium/gonadal** | Parathyroid/gonads | Stones/bones/groans/moans, libido, menses |

The unifying trick: most endocrine disease is a **too-much / too-little pair** with mirror-image symptoms. Learn one side and the other is its opposite — the mirror tables below are the fastest way to hold hyper vs hypo in your head.

> **See also** — Reproductive & Sexual History — the gonadal axis and menstrual/sexual symptoms

## The questions

### 1. Thyroid — hyper | hypo mirror — *"Do you feel the heat or cold more than others? Change in weight, bowels, mood, energy? Any neck swelling or trouble breathing/swallowing?"*

**Why we ask:** Thyroid hormone sets the metabolic rate, so its excess/deficiency touches nearly every system. Screening the paired cluster is more sensitive than any single symptom — and because the two states mirror each other, the pattern itself is the diagnosis.

| ⬆ Hyperthyroid | ⬇ Hypothyroid |
|---|---|
| Weight loss (good appetite) | Weight gain |
| Heat intolerance, sweating | Cold intolerance |
| Diarrhoea | Constipation |
| Anxious, restless, tremor | Tired, slowed thinking |
| Palpitations (AF) | Angina, bradycardia |
| Oligo/amenorrhoea | Menorrhagia |
| Warm sweaty skin | Dry skin, hair changes, hoarse voice |
| Eye changes (Graves) | Carpal tunnel, muscle cramps |

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **A visible/palpable neck lump moving on swallowing** → screen obstructive symptoms (shortness of breath, wheeze, stridor, dysphagia, voice change) and malignancy features (rapid growth, fixed lump, lymph nodes)

---

### 2. Diabetes — hyperglycaemia symptoms — *"Are you passing a lot of urine and drinking more than usual? Any unexplained weight loss or blurred vision?"*

**Why we ask:** The osmotic symptoms of hyperglycaemia are the classic presentation; the acute end (ketosis) is a red flag.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- Polyuria, polydipsia (thirst), nocturia
- Lethargy, weight change, blurred vision
- Recurrent infections (thrush, skin, urinary) and slow healing

**Red flag:** Rapid-onset ketotic symptoms → type 1 / DKA — an emergency.

---

### 3. Diabetes — hypoglycaemia symptoms — *"Do you ever go shaky, sweaty or confused, relieved by eating?"*

**Why we ask:** Only relevant if the patient is on insulin or a sulfonylurea — otherwise you can usually skip it. Where relevant, it drives safety and treatment adjustment.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- Autonomic: tremor, sweating, palpitations, hunger
- Neuroglycopenic: morning headaches, confusion, disturbance of conscious state, seizures
- Weight change (from defensive snacking)
- **Not on insulin or a sulfonylurea** → hypoglycaemia unlikely, can skip

---

### 4. Diabetes — complications screen — *"Has the diabetes affected your eyes, kidneys, nerves, feet or heart?"*

**Why we ask:** Long-term hyperglycaemia damages small and large vessels — screening the end-organs is core to every established-diabetes review.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- Microvascular: visual disturbance (retinopathy), renal dysfunction, peripheral neuropathy (numbness, foot ulcers)
- Macrovascular: heart disease, peripheral vascular disease, stroke
- Other: erectile dysfunction (early vascular + autonomic marker), non-healing ulcers

---

### 5. Known diabetes — the control frame — *"When were you diagnosed? How is it managed? How well controlled is it? Any complications?"*

**Why we ask:** For an established chronic disease you switch from screening to characterising. This four-part frame — timeframe → management → current control → complications — is a template you can reuse for almost any known chronic condition (asthma, IHD, IBD).

**Rules in / rules out:**
- Timeframe: when diagnosed, type 1 vs type 2
- Management: diet, oral agents, insulin (regimen), who monitors it
- Current control: HbA1c if known, home glucose readings, frequency of hypos, recent admissions
- Complications: run the complications screen

---

### 6. Adrenal — Cushing | Addison mirror — *"Persistent fatigue, dizziness on standing, or skin darkening? Or the opposite — central weight gain, easy bruising, purple stretch marks?"*

**Why we ask:** Rare but dangerous and easily missed; cortisol excess and deficiency look completely different. Ask about steroid use first — exogenous steroids are by far the commonest cause of Cushing's.

| ⬆ Cushing (excess) | ⬇ Addison (deficiency) |
|---|---|
| Central obesity, moon face | Weight loss |
| Purple striae, easy bruising | Hyperpigmentation (buccal, creases) |
| Hypertension | Postural hypotension, dizziness |
| Proximal muscle weakness | Fatigue, weakness |
| Mood change, hyperglycaemia | Salt craving, nausea; crisis = emergency |

**Rules in / rules out:**
- Ask about steroid use — the commonest cause of Cushing's

**Red flag:** Addisonian crisis (hypotension, collapse) is an emergency.

---

### 7. Headache & visual fields — *"Any headaches, or a change in your side (peripheral) vision?"*

**Why we ask:** A pituitary mass presses upward on the optic chiasm, classically producing bitemporal hemianopia — a mass effect independent of which hormone is over- or under-produced.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Bitemporal hemianopia + headache** → chiasmal compression

---

### 8. Acral growth (acromegaly) — *"Have your hands, feet or facial features changed — bigger rings, shoe size, jaw?"*

**Why we ask:** Excess growth hormone after epiphyseal closure thickens soft tissue and bone rather than causing height growth — the change is usually gradual enough that old photos are more revealing than the patient's own recollection.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Enlarging hands/feet (ring/shoe size), coarse features, sweating** → acromegaly

---

### 9. Galactorrhoea — *"Any milk discharge from the breasts?"*

**Why we ask:** Prolactin excess suppresses the gonadal axis, so galactorrhoea usually travels with amenorrhoea or low libido rather than appearing alone.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Galactorrhoea + amenorrhoea / low libido** → prolactinoma

---

### 10. Calcium — hyper | hypo mirror — *"Any kidney stones, bone pain, tummy pains, constipation, low mood — or tingling and muscle cramps?"*

**Why we ask:** Hypercalcaemia has a neat mnemonic — "stones, bones, abdominal groans, psychiatric moans" — and hypocalcaemia is the neuromuscular mirror.

| ⬆ Hypercalcaemia | ⬇ Hypocalcaemia |
|---|---|
| Renal stones, polyuria | Perioral / peripheral tingling |
| Bone pain | Muscle cramps, twitches, spasm (tetany) |
| Abdominal pain, constipation | — |
| Fatigue, confusion, low mood | Anxiety, irritability |

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Hypercalcaemia** → primary hyperparathyroidism or malignancy (pair with the weight-loss/cancer screen)
- **Hypocalcaemia** → post-thyroid/parathyroid surgery, vitamin D deficiency, CKD

---

### 11. Gonadal / reproductive — *"Change in libido, sexual function or periods? Hot flushes? Change in body or facial hair?"*

**Why we ask:** Sex-steroid axes overlap with thyroid, pituitary and adrenal disease; changes here often surface an endocrine cause. (For the full reproductive and sexual history, see the Reproductive system file.)

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Oligo/amenorrhoea + hirsutism + acne + weight** → PCOS
- **Hot flushes + irregular then absent periods** → menopause
- **Low libido + fatigue + loss of body hair** → hypogonadism

---

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

THYROID: hyper|hypo mirror (weight, temperature, bowels, cardiac, menstrual, skin) + goitre/obstructive · GLUCOSE: hyperglycaemia symptoms, hypoglycaemia (if on insulin), complications, known-diabetes frame (timeframe/management/control/complications) · ADRENAL: Cushing|Addison mirror (steroid use?) · PITUITARY: headache/visual fields, acral growth, galactorrhoea · CALCIUM: hyper|hypo mirror (stones/bones/groans/moans vs tingling/tetany) · GONADAL: libido, menses, hirsutism. Most endocrine disease is a too-much/too-little pair.
