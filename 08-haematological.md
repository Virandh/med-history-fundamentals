# Haematological Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

Blood has three jobs — carry oxygen, fight infection, and clot. Disease is failure of one of the three, plus the reticuloendothelial signs:

> **CARRY → CLOT → FIGHT → NODES/ORGANS**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Carry** | Red cells | Fatigue, breathlessness, pallor, dizziness |
| **Clot** | Platelets / coagulation | Easy bruising, bleeding, clots (DVT/PE) |
| **Fight** | White cells | Recurrent/severe infections, fevers |
| **Nodes/organs** | Reticuloendothelial | Lumps (lymphadenopathy), abdominal fullness (spleen), night sweats, weight loss, itch, bone pain |

Haematology overlaps heavily with the **constitutional screen** (B symptoms) — the two are best learned together. A failure of *all three* cell lines (anaemia + bleeding + infections) points to marrow failure.

> **See also** — General / Constitutional — the B-symptom screen (weight loss, fever, night sweats)

## The questions

### 1. Anaemia symptoms — *"Do you feel tired, breathless on exertion, or dizzy on standing? Have people said you look pale?"*

**Why we ask:** Anaemia reduces oxygen delivery, producing a predictable symptom cluster; the job then is to find why (blood loss, deficiency, haemolysis, marrow).

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How much can you do before you're tired or breathless — has that changed?"* — Quantifies functional impact and gives a severity/trajectory baseline
- **!** *"Any bleeding from the bowel, black stools, or vomited blood?"* — GI blood loss is the commonest cause of iron-deficiency anaemia in men and postmenopausal women — right-sided colon cancer classically presents this way
- *"For women — are your periods heavy or frequent?"* — The commonest cause of iron deficiency in women of reproductive age
- *"What's your diet like — any chance you're low in iron, B12, or folate?"* — Vegan/vegetarian diet → B12; poor intake generally → iron/folate; ask about prior stomach or bowel surgery too (reduced absorption)
- *"Any yellowing of the skin or eyes, or dark urine?"* — Jaundice alongside anaemia raises haemolysis rather than a nutritional or bleeding cause
- *"Any chronic illness — kidney disease, rheumatoid arthritis, or another inflammatory condition?"* — Anaemia of chronic disease is a diagnosis of exclusion once bleeding and deficiency are ruled out
- **!** *"Any bone pain, recurrent infections, or unusual bruising alongside the tiredness?"* — A failure of all three cell lines together points to marrow failure/leukaemia rather than simple anaemia
- *"Any recent travel, especially somewhere with malaria?"* — Chronic malarial or parasitic infection (e.g. hookworm) is a common cause of chronic anaemia globally

> Full work-up → **tiredness** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Fatigue + exertional dyspnoea + pallor ± palpitations, postural dizziness** → anaemia; then hunt the cause:
- Ask about blood loss: heavy periods (women — the commonest cause of iron deficiency), GI bleeding (melaena, PR blood), and the change-in-bowel-habit/weight-loss cancer screen (right-sided colon cancer classically presents as iron-deficiency anaemia)
- **Diet (vegan** → B12), pica and restless legs (iron deficiency), jaundice (haemolysis)
- Ask about GI symptoms of malabsorption (coeliac) and known chronic disease (renal, inflammatory)
- **Anaemia + bruising + recurrent infections together** → marrow failure, not simple anaemia — a failure of all three cell lines

**Red flag:** Iron-deficiency anaemia in a man or postmenopausal woman is a GI cancer until excluded.

### 2. Mouth ulcers — *"Any mouth ulcers?"*

**Why we ask:** Recurrent or severe mouth ulcers can be a mucosal bleeding-tendency sign, or reflect the neutropenia of marrow failure — worth asking separately rather than folding into 'bruising'.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How many at once, and how often do they come back?"* — Multiple or unusually frequent ulcers raise a systemic cause rather than isolated benign aphthous ulcers
- *"Any other bleeding, bruising, or infections around the same time?"* — Combined with other cytopenic signs, mouth ulcers can reflect the neutropenia of marrow failure rather than a standalone problem

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Isolated, recurrent, no other bleeding/infection signs** → usually benign aphthous ulcers

---

### 3. Bruising & bleeding — *"Do you bruise easily or bleed more than expected — gums, nosebleeds, after cuts? Blood in urine or stool?"*

**Why we ask:** The pattern of bleeding separates platelet/vascular from coagulation-factor disease — see the discriminator table below.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Where do you bruise or bleed — skin and gums, or deep into joints and muscles?"* — Skin/mucosal (petechiae, gum/nose bleeding) → platelet problem; deep bleeding into joints/muscles → coagulation factor problem
- *"Does bleeding start immediately after a cut, or is it delayed?"* — Immediate → platelet/vascular; delayed after trauma → coagulation factor
- *"Are the bruises small and numerous, or large single ones?"* — Small, numerous, superficial → platelet; large, deep haematomas → coagulation factor
- *"Any blood in your urine or stool, or heavy periods?"* — Extends the bleeding survey beyond skin — mucosal bleeding elsewhere supports the same platelet/vascular pattern
- *"Are you on aspirin, another blood thinner, or anti-inflammatory tablets?"* — Medication is a common, reversible cause — always check before assuming an intrinsic bleeding disorder
- *"Any family history of bleeding problems?"* — Raises an inherited disorder (haemophilia, von Willebrand disease) — often only diagnosed after unusual bleeding at surgery
- **!** *"Any fatigue, fevers, or recurrent infections alongside the bruising?"* — New widespread bruising + fatigue + infections → marrow failure/leukaemia, not an isolated platelet problem
- *"Have you ever had your spleen removed?"* — Splenectomy can increase platelet numbers (thrombocytosis), changing the picture rather than causing bleeding

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Mucocutaneous bleeding — petechiae, purpura, gum/nose bleeding, menorrhagia** → platelet problem (thrombocytopenia, e.g. ITP; or dysfunction)
- **Deep bleeding — into joints/muscles, delayed bleeding after trauma** → coagulation factor problem (haemophilia, warfarin, DIC, liver disease)
- Ask about anticoagulants/antiplatelets and family history of bleeding

**Red flag:** New widespread bruising + fatigue + infections → marrow failure/leukaemia.

### 4. Thrombosis — *"Ever had a clot in the leg or lung? Any leg swelling or pain? Recent flights, surgery or immobility?"*

**Why we ask:** The clotting system can also over-perform. Screens for VTE and thrombophilia, and connects to the constitutional/malignancy screen (cancer is prothrombotic).

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Any swelling, pain, or redness in one calf?"* — Unilateral calf swelling/pain → DVT
- **!** *"Any sudden breathlessness or sharp chest pain with it?"* — Suggests extension to pulmonary embolism
- **!** *"Has this happened before, unprovoked, or in an unusual place, or is there a family history?"* — Recurrent, unprovoked, or unusual-site thrombosis raises an inherited thrombophilia — and unprovoked VTE can itself be the first sign of an occult cancer
- *"Any recent surgery, long flight, immobility, or hormone treatment (pill, HRT, pregnancy)?"* — The common provoking factors — worth identifying because they change how aggressively you look for an underlying cause
- *"Do you smoke, or are you overweight?"* — Additional modifiable risk factors that compound the inherited and provoked risks

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Unilateral calf swelling/pain** → DVT; with pleuritic pain/dyspnoea → PE
- **Recurrent or unprovoked VTE, or in unusual sites, or family history** → thrombophilia; also an occult-cancer trigger
- Ask about provoking factors: surgery, immobility, long flights, oestrogens, pregnancy

---

### 5. Infection susceptibility — *"Do you get frequent, severe or unusual infections that take a long time to clear?"*

**Why we ask:** Screens white-cell number/function and immune competence. Recurrent severe infection can be the presentation of leukaemia, marrow failure, or immunodeficiency.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How often, how severe, and how long do they take to clear compared with before?"* — Frequency, severity and clearance time together are more telling than any single infection
- *"Any particular type recurring — chest, sinus, skin, or urinary?"* — The pattern of infection type can point to a specific immune defect
- **!** *"Any fatigue, bruising, or weight loss alongside the infections?"* — Frequent severe bacterial infections + fatigue + bruising → marrow failure/leukaemia
- *"Any risk factors for HIV — could we talk about your sexual history or drug use?"* — Recurrent infections + weight loss + risk factors → HIV; ask sensitively but directly

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Frequent severe bacterial infections + fatigue + bruising** → marrow failure/leukaemia
- **Recurrent infections + weight loss + risk factors** → HIV — ask sensitively about risk
- Recurrent specific infection types can point to specific immune defects

---

### 6. Lymphadenopathy — *"Any lumps in the neck, armpits or groin?"*

**Why we ask:** Painless, rubbery, persistent nodes with B symptoms are the hallmark of lymphoma; tender nodes with a recent infection are usually reactive.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Are the lumps painful or tender, and did they come on with an infection nearby?"* — Tender, with a recent local infection → usually reactive; painless → warrants more concern
- *"Are they firm and rubbery, and have they been there for weeks without shrinking?"* — Painless, rubbery, persistent nodes are the textbook lymphoma pattern
- **!** *"Any night sweats, weight loss, fevers, or itching alongside the lumps?"* — The B-symptom cluster alongside lymphadenopathy strengthens concern for lymphoma

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Painless rubbery persistent nodes + drenching night sweats + weight loss + itch** → lymphoma (the B-symptom cluster)
- **Tender, associated with a local infection** → reactive

---

### 7. Splenic fullness / early satiety — *"Any fullness or discomfort in the upper-left abdomen, or filling up quickly when you eat?"*

**Why we ask:** The spleen enlarges quietly — early satiety from it pressing on the stomach is often the only clue before it's felt on examination.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it on the left side, under your ribs, and does it fill up quickly when you eat?"* — Left-upper-quadrant fullness with early satiety is the classic (if subtle) symptom of a spleen enlarging before it's felt on examination
- *"Any fatigue, bruising, or night sweats alongside it?"* — Points to the underlying cause — myeloproliferative disease, lymphoma, or haemolysis — rather than a GI explanation

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Left-upper-quadrant fullness/early satiety** → splenomegaly (myeloproliferative disease, lymphoma, haemolysis)

---

### 8. Constitutional (B) symptoms — *"Any night sweats, weight loss, or generalised itching?"*

**Why we ask:** This triad — the classic 'B symptoms' — marks lymphoproliferative and myeloproliferative disease as systemic, not just a nodal or marrow problem; itch after a hot shower with a ruddy complexion specifically suggests polycythaemia.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Are the night sweats drenching enough to need changing the sheets?"* — Drenching, not just feeling warm at night, is the specific pattern that matters for the B-symptom cluster
- *"Is the itch worse after a hot shower, and do you have a ruddy or flushed complexion?"* — This specific pattern points to polycythaemia rather than the lymphoma-associated itch
- *"Any lumps, bone pain, or unexplained bruising alongside these?"* — Ties the constitutional symptoms back to a nodal, marrow, or myeloproliferative source

> Full work-up → **weight-loss** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Drenching night sweats + weight loss + itch** → the B-symptom cluster (lymphoma)
- **Generalised itch (esp. after a hot shower) + ruddy complexion** → polycythaemia

---

### 9. Bone pain — *"Any persistent bone pain?"*

**Why we ask:** Marrow infiltration or expansion causes bone pain directly — in an older patient with fatigue and recurrent infection it raises myeloma.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it in one spot, or several bones, and is it worse at night or with movement?"* — Multifocal bone pain, worse with movement, in an older adult raises myeloma
- **!** *"Any thirst, constipation, or confusion alongside it?"* — Symptoms of hypercalcaemia (part of the CRAB criteria) support myeloma as the underlying cause

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Bone pain + fatigue + recurrent infection + thirst/constipation (hypercalcaemia) in an older patient** → myeloma

## Discriminator table: platelet/vascular vs coagulation-factor bleeding

| Clue | Platelet / vascular | Coagulation factor |
|---|---|---|
| Site | Skin & mucosa (petechiae, purpura, gums, epistaxis, menorrhagia) | Deep — joints (haemarthrosis), muscles |
| Timing after injury | Immediate | Delayed |
| Bruises | Small, superficial | Large, palpable haematomas |
| Examples | ITP, thrombocytopenia, von Willebrand, aspirin | Haemophilia, warfarin, DIC, liver disease |

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

CARRY: fatigue, exertional dyspnoea, pallor, dizziness — then find the cause (blood loss? diet? haemolysis?) · CLOT: easy bruising/bleeding (mucocutaneous vs deep), anticoagulants; thrombosis (DVT/PE, provokers) · FIGHT: recurrent/severe/unusual infections · NODES/ORGANS: lymphadenopathy, splenic fullness, night sweats, weight loss, itch, bone pain
