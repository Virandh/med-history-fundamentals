# Gastrointestinal Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

Follow the food from mouth to anus — the GI screen is literally a journey down the tube:

> **TOP → MIDDLE → BOTTOM → OUTSIDE (liver)**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Top** | Mouth, oesophagus, stomach | Swallowing, heartburn, nausea/vomiting, haematemesis |
| **Middle** | Abdomen generally | Abdominal pain, appetite/weight, bloating/postprandial fullness |
| **Bottom** | Small/large bowel, rectum | Bowel habit change, blood/melaena, mucus |
| **Outside** | Hepatobiliary | Jaundice, dark urine/pale stool, itch |

Move top-to-bottom and you cannot forget a segment. Two red-flag threads run the whole length: **bleeding** (haematemesis at the top → melaena → fresh PR blood at the bottom) and **obstruction/malignancy** (dysphagia at the top → change in bowel habit at the bottom).

> **See also** — Haematological — GI blood loss is the commonest cause of iron-deficiency anaemia

## The questions

### 1. Dysphagia — *"Any difficulty swallowing? Does food or drink stick — solids, liquids, or both?"*

**Why we ask:** Dysphagia is always significant and the solid-vs-liquid pattern localises the lesion.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Do you have trouble with solids, liquids, or both?"* — Solids only → mechanical (cancer, stricture) · solids and liquids together → a motor problem (achalasia, spasm)
- *"Where does it feel like it's sticking — can you point?"* — Pointing to the lower oesophagus supports a mechanical obstruction there — but referred sensation is unreliable for lesions higher up
- *"Is it constant, or does it come and go — worse with the first few mouthfuls?"* — Intermittent, or worse on first swallows, → eosinophilic oesophagitis, a lower oesophageal ring, or (rarely) diffuse oesophageal spasm
- **!** *"Has it been getting steadily worse?"* — Progressive difficulty → stricture, carcinoma, or achalasia
- *"Do you cough or choke as you start to swallow, or does fluid come back up your nose?"* — Suggests oropharyngeal dysphagia — a pharyngeal or neurological (bulbar) cause, not an oesophageal one
- *"Is it painful to swallow?"* — Odynophagia suggests acute inflammation — infective oesophagitis (Candida, herpes), a lodged pill, or caustic injury
- *"Any heartburn or acid regurgitation with it?"* — Supports GORD, with or without a peptic stricture, as the cause
- **!** *"Have you lost weight? Do you have asthma or hay fever?"* — Weight loss raises concern for cancer; atopy supports eosinophilic oesophagitis

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Solids first, progressing to liquids** → mechanical obstruction: oesophageal cancer (esp. with weight loss, older, smoker/drinker) or benign stricture
- **Solids and liquids from the start** → motility disorder (achalasia, neuromuscular)
- **Painful swallowing (odynophagia)** → oesophagitis/candida
- **Difficulty initiating the swallow with coughing/nasal regurgitation** → oropharyngeal/neurological (stroke, bulbar)

**Red flag:** Progressive dysphagia + weight loss → oesophageal cancer until excluded.

### 2. Heartburn / dyspepsia — *"Any heartburn, acid reflux, or burning discomfort in the upper abdomen?"*

**Why we ask:** Very common; the job is to separate benign reflux from the ulcers and the cancers.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How often does this happen — more than once a week?"* — More than weekly supports a diagnosis of GORD rather than occasional reflux
- *"Does it come on after meals, or when you bend forward or lie flat?"* — Typical triggers for acid reflux
- **!** *"Does it radiate across your chest, down your arm, or into your jaw?"* — Raises concern for myocardial ischaemia rather than reflux — don't anchor on GORD until this is excluded
- *"Is it relieved by antacids, or does it come with a sudden rush of bitter fluid into your mouth?"* — Relief with antacids and true acid regurgitation both support reflux; a salty or tasteless fluid instead is waterbrash, not GORD
- **!** *"Any trouble swallowing, weight loss, or black stools/vomited blood with it?"* — The ALARM features that mean this isn't straightforward reflux and needs endoscopy

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Retrosternal burning, worse lying flat/after meals, relieved by antacids** → GORD
- **Epigastric pain related to meals** → peptic ulcer (the classic "duodenal relieved by food, gastric worsened by it" pattern is unreliable — Talley: duodenal and gastric ulcers can't be distinguished clinically, and pain isn't always meal-related)
- **Dyspepsia + ALARM features (Anaemia, Loss of weight, Anorexia, Recent onset/progressive, Melaena/haematemesis, Swallowing difficulty)** → urgent endoscopy for gastric cancer (Talley's core alarm set is the four: dysphagia, weight loss, melaena, haematemesis)
- **Radiates across the chest, down the arm, or into the jaw** → raises myocardial ischaemia instead of reflux

**Red flag:** ALARM: Anaemia, Loss of weight, Anorexia, Recent/progressive onset, Melaena/haematemesis, Swallowing difficulty.

### 3. Nausea and vomiting — *"Any nausea or vomiting? How often, and what seems to trigger it?"*

**Why we ask:** Timing and content localise the cause — early-morning vomiting points away from the gut itself.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Does it happen right after eating, or hours later?"* — Delayed more than an hour after meals → gastric outlet obstruction or gastroparesis · early morning, before eating, → pregnancy, alcohol, or raised intracranial pressure
- **!** *"What does the vomit look like — bile-stained, old food, or blood?"* — Bile → an open duodenal–gastric connection · old food → outlet obstruction · blood → ulceration, needing its own work-up
- *"Any abdominal pain, colicky pain with distension, or absence of flatus?"* — Points towards bowel obstruction rather than a primary vomiting disorder
- *"Is it actually vomiting, or does food come back up effortlessly and get re-swallowed or spat out?"* — Effortless regurgitation of recently eaten food is rumination, not true vomiting — a different diagnosis entirely
- *"Any headaches, or is this a recurring pattern with well periods in between?"* — Worsening headaches raise raised intracranial pressure; distinct well-then-vomiting cycles suggest cyclical vomiting syndrome

> Full work-up → **nausea-vomiting** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Vomiting + colicky pain + distension + absolute constipation** → bowel obstruction
- **Faeculent vomiting** → distal/large bowel obstruction or fistula
- **Early morning vomiting** → raised ICP, pregnancy, alcohol

### 4. Haematemesis — *"What does the vomit look like — any blood or coffee-grounds?"*

**Why we ask:** Haematemesis is a red flag on its own — it always needs an upper GI source excluded, and the character (fresh blood vs coffee-grounds) hints at the rate of bleeding.

**If yes, ask:**
- **!** *"Was it fresh red blood, or did it look like coffee grounds?"* — Fresh blood suggests a brisk, ongoing bleed; coffee-grounds suggests blood altered by acid — a slower or older bleed
- *"Did the blood come with the first vomit, or only after repeated retching?"* — Blood appearing only after several episodes of clear vomiting suggests a Mallory–Weiss tear rather than a primary ulcer bleed
- *"Are you on aspirin, NSAIDs, or steroids, and do you drink alcohol or have known liver disease?"* — The core risk factors for peptic ulcer bleeding and for varices

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Haematemesis / coffee-ground vomit** → upper GI bleed (ulcer, varices, Mallory–Weiss); ask about NSAIDs, alcohol, known liver disease

**Red flag:** Haematemesis — ask about NSAIDs, alcohol, known liver disease.

---

### 5. Abdominal pain — *"Any tummy pain? Where exactly? Any bloating or fullness after meals?"*

**Why we ask:** Site and character map onto the organ and process — characterise fully with SOCRATES. Bloating/postprandial fullness point to functional or gastric causes.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Can you point to exactly where it is, and does it move anywhere else?"* — Site follows embryological origin — epigastric (foregut), periumbilical (midgut), suprapubic (hindgut); radiation to the back suggests pancreas, to the scapula suggests gallbladder/diaphragm
- *"Does it come in waves, or is it constant?"* — Colicky (waves) → obstruction (bowel, ureter) · steady/constant → inflammation or ischaemia
- *"Is it related to eating — worse after meals, or when fasting?"* — Meal-related epigastric pain suggests peptic disease, though the link isn't reliable; pain specifically provoked by eating can also be intestinal ischaemia
- *"Do you feel better moving around, or do you want to lie perfectly still?"* — Restlessness/rolling around → colicky pain (obstruction); lying still, reluctant to move → peritonitis
- *"Does passing wind or a bowel motion ease it?"* — Temporary relief with defecation/flatus points to colonic disease
- *"Any fever, jaundice, or vomiting with it?"* — Fever + jaundice + RUQ pain → cholangitis; vomiting is common with biliary and pancreatic pain
- **!** *"Did it come on suddenly and severely — the worst pain you've ever had?"* — Sudden, maximal-at-onset, severe pain with shock → perforation, ruptured AAA, mesenteric ischaemia, ruptured ectopic
- **!** *"Any change in bowel habit, blood in the stool, or weight loss alongside it?"* — Reframes chronic abdominal pain as a possible malignancy work-up rather than a functional one

> Full work-up → **abdominal-pain** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **RUQ/epigastric, typically constant and severe for hours (Talley: biliary pain is "rarely colicky"; the fatty-food trigger is unpredictable)** → biliary pain/cholecystitis; constant + fever + jaundice → cholangitis
- **Epigastric boring to the back, relieved leaning forward** → pancreatitis; also peptic ulcer, AAA
- **RIF — migratory from central** → appendicitis
- LIF — diverticulitis, colitis
- **Loin to groin, colicky, can't stay still** → renal colic
- **Central, poorly localised, then localising** → the visceral-to-parietal progression of many surgical abdomens

**Red flag:** Sudden "worst ever" + shock → perforation, ruptured AAA, mesenteric ischaemia, ruptured ectopic.

### 6. Appetite and weight — *"How is your appetite? Any weight loss?"*

**Why we ask:** Ties GI complaints to the constitutional malignancy screen; weight loss escalates the urgency of every symptom above.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Has your appetite changed — more, less, or the same?"* — Loss of appetite + weight loss raises concern for malignancy; weight loss with an increased appetite suggests malabsorption or a hypermetabolic state (e.g. thyrotoxicosis)
- *"How much weight, and over what period?"* — Quantify — document onset and rate; rapid, unintentional loss is what escalates urgency
- *"Do you fill up quickly, or feel uncomfortably full after normal-sized meals?"* — Early satiety/postprandial fullness can be gastric cancer, peptic ulcer, or functional dyspepsia
- *"Any change in how food tastes, or reason you might be eating less — low mood, dentition, swallowing?"* — Taste disturbance can occur with liver disease; screens for non-GI explanations before assuming malignancy

> Full work-up → **weight-loss** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Anorexia + weight loss together** → raises suspicion of underlying malignancy (but also occurs in depression and other disease)
- **Weight loss with an increased appetite** → malabsorption or a hypermetabolic state, e.g. thyrotoxicosis
- **Early satiety/postprandial fullness without weight loss** → functional dyspepsia

### 7. Change in bowel habit — *"Have your bowels changed — more or less often, looser or harder than normal for you?"*

**Why we ask:** "Normal" is individual, so anchor to the patient's baseline. A persistent change is a cancer screen.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"What's changed, exactly — more or less often, looser or harder, compared to your normal?"* — Anchor to the patient's own baseline — "normal" is individual
- *"How long has this been going on, and did it come on suddenly or gradually?"* — Acute → more likely infective; chronic/persistent needs a systematic work-up
- **!** *"Any blood, mucus, or pus in the stool? Do you have to rush to the toilet?"* — Blood/mucus/pus and urgency point to colonic inflammation — IBD or infective colitis
- **!** *"Any weight loss, or waking at night specifically because of your bowels?"* — Weight loss or nocturnal symptoms are organic red flags — they don't fit a purely functional diagnosis
- *"Any recent antibiotics, or travel?"* — Antibiotics → consider C. difficile; travel → consider Giardia and other infective causes
- *"Is any pain relieved by opening your bowels, and does it come with bloating or a feeling of incomplete emptying?"* — Supports IBS by the Rome criteria — a positive diagnosis, not one of exclusion, once red flags are absent
- *"Any family history of bowel cancer, coeliac disease, or inflammatory bowel disease?"* — Shifts pre-test probability and may prompt earlier investigation
- *"Any joint pains, mouth ulcers, or eye redness alongside the bowel symptoms?"* — Extra-intestinal features support inflammatory bowel disease

> Full work-up → **abnormal-stools** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Persistent change (esp. looser/more frequent) + weight loss + PR bleeding, especially in an older adult** → colorectal cancer until excluded (symptomatic suspicion is age-independent; note Australia's bowel-screening age is now 45)
- **Diarrhoea + blood + mucus + urgency + systemic upset in a young patient** → IBD
- **Alternating constipation/diarrhoea with pain relieved by defecation, no red flags** → IBS — a positive diagnosis on the Rome criteria (recurrent abdominal pain ≥1 day/week for 3 months + ≥2 of: related to defecation, change in stool frequency, change in stool form), no longer a diagnosis of exclusion
- **Explosive diarrhoea after travel/antibiotics** → infective

### 8. Rectal bleeding / melaena — *"Any blood when you go to the toilet — bright red, or black and tarry?"*

**Why we ask:** Colour tells you the level of the bleed.

**If yes, ask:**
- **!** *"Is the blood bright red or black and tarry?"* — Bright red → usually a lower source · black, tarry, offensive (melaena) → usually an upper GI bleed
- *"Is it on the paper/coating the stool, or mixed all through it?"* — On the surface/paper → anorectal (haemorrhoids, fissure) · mixed through the stool → a more proximal colonic source, including cancer
- **!** *"Any change in bowel habit or weight loss alongside it?"* — Blood + altered habit + weight loss → colorectal cancer until excluded
- *"Have you vomited any blood or coffee-ground material?"* — Pairs melaena with the haematemesis question — confirms an upper GI source
- *"Any abdominal pain with it?"* — Painless vs pain-associated bleeding narrows the differential (e.g. painless diverticular bleed vs painful colitis)
- *"Any family or personal history of polyps, bowel cancer, or inflammatory bowel disease?"* — Raises pre-test probability and may change the urgency of investigation
- *"Are you on aspirin, other blood thinners, or NSAIDs?"* — These provoke and worsen GI bleeding from any source and change the immediate risk

> Full work-up → **abnormal-stools** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Melaena (black, tarry, offensive)** → upper GI bleed — pair with the haematemesis question
- **Bright red coating the stool / on the paper** → anorectal (haemorrhoids, fissure)
- **Blood mixed through the stool + change in habit + weight loss** → colorectal cancer

---

### 9. Mucus — *"Any mucus or slime with your stool?"*

**Why we ask:** Mucus with blood and diarrhoea points to mucosal inflammation rather than a purely mechanical or bleeding cause.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Bloody diarrhoea with mucus** → colitis (IBD or infective)

---

### 10. Steatorrhoea — *"Is the stool pale, greasy, or hard to flush?"*

**Why we ask:** Pale, greasy, floating stool means fat isn't being absorbed — the fat itself is passing through undigested.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Do you see oil droplets in the toilet, or does the stool float and stick to the bowl?"* — Oil droplets suggest chronic pancreatitis specifically; floating and hard-to-flush is the general fat-malabsorption pattern
- *"Any weight loss, or a personal/family history of coeliac disease?"* — Helps distinguish pancreatic insufficiency, biliary obstruction, and coeliac disease as the three big causes

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Pale, greasy, floating, hard-to-flush stool** → fat malabsorption (pancreatic insufficiency, biliary obstruction, coeliac disease)

---

### 11. Jaundice — *"Any yellowing of your eyes or skin?"*

**Why we ask:** Jaundice is the headline sign of hepatobiliary disease — the accompanying urine/stool/itch pattern (asked next) tells you which type before any blood test.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is your urine darker than usual, and are your stools pale?"* — Dark urine + pale stools → an obstructive (post-hepatic) picture
- *"Any itching?"* — Itch tracks with the same cholestatic/obstructive picture
- **!** *"Any fever, or abdominal pain?"* — Fever + RUQ pain + jaundice → cholangitis, needs urgent treatment
- **!** *"Any change in appetite or weight loss?"* — Painless jaundice with weight loss raises pancreatic head cancer (Courvoisier)
- **!** *"Any vomiting blood, or black stools?"* — Suggests coagulopathy from liver disease, or a separate GI bleed — needs its own work-up
- *"How much alcohol do you drink, and for how long? Any past hepatitis, or new medications?"* — The core drug/alcohol/viral triad for a hepatic cause
- *"Any tattoos, IV drug use, blood transfusions, or high-risk sexual contact?"* — Risk factors for viral hepatitis B/C
- *"Any recent travel to areas where hepatitis is common?"* — Hepatitis A exposure risk

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Jaundice + risk factors (alcohol, viral hepatitis, paracetamol, travel) + systemic upset** → hepatic
- **Jaundice ± anaemia, normal urine/stool** → pre-hepatic (haemolysis)
- **Fever + abdominal pain + jaundice** → cholangitis, needs urgent treatment
- **Painless jaundice + weight loss** → think pancreatic head cancer (Courvoisier) until excluded

---

### 12. Dark urine / pale stool — *"Any change in the colour of your urine or stool — dark urine, pale or clay-coloured stool?"*

**Why we ask:** Conjugated bilirubin being shunted into the urine instead of the gut is the signature of obstructive (post-hepatic) jaundice.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Any yellowing of your eyes or skin, or itching?"* — Confirms this sits within the same obstructive/cholestatic picture as jaundice, rather than an isolated urine colour change
- *"Any abdominal pain, or unexplained weight loss?"* — Painful → gallstones; painless with weight loss → think pancreatic head cancer (Courvoisier)

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Dark urine + pale stools** → obstructive (post-hepatic) jaundice: gallstones, or if painless with weight loss, pancreatic head cancer (Courvoisier)

---

### 13. Itch (pruritus) — *"Any itching, especially generalised?"*

**Why we ask:** Retained bile salts under the skin cause the itch of cholestasis — it tracks with the same obstructive picture as dark urine and pale stool.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it generalised, or worse in particular areas — like your hands and feet?"* — Generalised itch, worse over the extremities, is typical of cholestatic liver disease
- *"Any yellowing of the eyes/skin, dark urine, or pale stools with it?"* — Confirms the cholestatic picture rather than a primary skin cause (eczema, scabies, dry skin)

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Generalised itch with jaundice** → cholestasis (obstructive or hepatic)

## Discriminator table: upper vs lower GI bleed

| Clue | Upper GI bleed | Lower GI bleed |
|---|---|---|
| Vomiting | Haematemesis / coffee-ground | Absent |
| Stool | Melaena (black, tarry, offensive) | Fresh or dark red PR blood |
| Common causes | Peptic ulcer, varices, Mallory–Weiss | Diverticular, haemorrhoids, cancer, colitis, angiodysplasia |
| Ask about | NSAIDs, alcohol, liver disease | Change in bowel habit, weight loss |
| Caveat | — | A brisk upper GI bleed can present as fresh PR blood + shock |

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

**TOP:** dysphagia (solids→liquids?), heartburn/dyspepsia (ALARM?), nausea/vomiting (blood?), haematemesis · **MIDDLE:** abdominal pain (SOCRATES + site), appetite/weight · **BOTTOM:** change in bowel habit, PR bleeding/melaena, mucus · **OUTSIDE:** jaundice + dark urine/pale stool/itch
