# Gastrointestinal Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

Follow the food from mouth to anus — the GI screen is literally a journey down the tube:

> **TOP → MIDDLE → BOTTOM → OUTSIDE (liver)**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Top** | Mouth, oesophagus, stomach | Swallowing, heartburn, nausea/vomiting, haematemesis |
| **Middle** | Abdomen generally | Abdominal pain, appetite/weight, bloating/postprandial fullness |
| **Bottom** | Small/large bowel, rectum | Bowel habit change, blood/melaena, mucus |
| **Outside** | Hepatobiliary | Jaundice, dark urine/pale stool, itch |

Move top-to-bottom and you cannot forget a segment. Two red-flag threads run the whole length: **bleeding** (haematemesis at the top → melaena → fresh PR blood at the bottom) and **obstruction/malignancy** (dysphagia at the top → change in bowel habit at the bottom).

> **See also** — Haematological — GI blood loss is the commonest cause of iron-deficiency anaemia

## The questions

### 1. Dysphagia — *"Any difficulty swallowing? Does food or drink stick — solids, liquids, or both?"*

**Why we ask:** Dysphagia is always significant and the solid-vs-liquid pattern localises the lesion.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Solids first, progressing to liquids** → mechanical obstruction: oesophageal cancer (esp. with weight loss, older, smoker/drinker) or benign stricture
- **Solids and liquids from the start** → motility disorder (achalasia, neuromuscular)
- **Painful swallowing (odynophagia)** → oesophagitis/candida
- **Difficulty initiating the swallow with coughing/nasal regurgitation** → oropharyngeal/neurological (stroke, bulbar)

**Red flag:** Progressive dysphagia + weight loss → oesophageal cancer until excluded.

---

### 2. Heartburn / dyspepsia — *"Any heartburn, acid reflux, or burning discomfort in the upper abdomen?"*

**Why we ask:** Very common; the job is to separate benign reflux from the ulcers and the cancers.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Retrosternal burning, worse lying flat/after meals, relieved by antacids** → GORD
- **Epigastric pain related to meals** → peptic ulcer (the classic "duodenal relieved by food, gastric worsened by it" pattern is unreliable — Talley: duodenal and gastric ulcers can't be distinguished clinically, and pain isn't always meal-related)
- **Dyspepsia + ALARM features (Anaemia, Loss of weight, Anorexia, Recent onset/progressive, Melaena/haematemesis, Swallowing difficulty)** → urgent endoscopy for gastric cancer (Talley's core alarm set is the four: dysphagia, weight loss, melaena, haematemesis)

**Red flag:** ALARM: Anaemia, Loss of weight, Anorexia, Recent/progressive onset, Melaena/haematemesis, Swallowing difficulty.

---

### 3. Nausea and vomiting — *"Any nausea or vomiting? What does the vomit look like — any blood or coffee-grounds?"*

**Why we ask:** Timing and content localise the cause; the appearance screens for upper GI bleeding.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Haematemesis / coffee-ground vomit** → upper GI bleed (ulcer, varices, Mallory–Weiss) — a red flag; ask about NSAIDs, alcohol, known liver disease
- **Vomiting + colicky pain + distension + absolute constipation** → bowel obstruction
- **Faeculent vomiting** → distal/large bowel obstruction or fistula
- **Early morning vomiting** → raised ICP, pregnancy, alcohol

**Red flag:** Haematemesis — ask about NSAIDs, alcohol, known liver disease.

---

### 4. Abdominal pain — *"Any tummy pain? Where exactly? Any bloating or fullness after meals?"*

**Why we ask:** Site and character map onto the organ and process — characterise fully with SOCRATES. Bloating/postprandial fullness point to functional or gastric causes.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **RUQ/epigastric, typically constant and severe for hours (Talley: biliary pain is "rarely colicky"; the fatty-food trigger is unpredictable)** → biliary pain/cholecystitis; constant + fever + jaundice → cholangitis
- **Epigastric boring to the back, relieved leaning forward** → pancreatitis; also peptic ulcer, AAA
- **RIF — migratory from central** → appendicitis
- LIF — diverticulitis, colitis
- **Loin to groin, colicky, can't stay still** → renal colic
- **Central, poorly localised, then localising** → the visceral-to-parietal progression of many surgical abdomens

**Red flag:** Sudden "worst ever" + shock → perforation, ruptured AAA, mesenteric ischaemia, ruptured ectopic.

---

### 5. Change in bowel habit — *"Have your bowels changed — more or less often, looser or harder than normal for you?"*

**Why we ask:** "Normal" is individual, so anchor to the patient's baseline. A persistent change is a cancer screen.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Persistent change (esp. looser/more frequent) + weight loss + PR bleeding, especially in an older adult** → colorectal cancer until excluded (symptomatic suspicion is age-independent; note Australia's bowel-screening age is now 45)
- **Diarrhoea + blood + mucus + urgency + systemic upset in a young patient** → IBD
- **Alternating constipation/diarrhoea with pain relieved by defecation, no red flags** → IBS — a positive diagnosis on the Rome criteria (recurrent abdominal pain ≥1 day/week for 3 months + ≥2 of: related to defecation, change in stool frequency, change in stool form), no longer a diagnosis of exclusion
- **Explosive diarrhoea after travel/antibiotics** → infective

---

### 6. Rectal bleeding / melaena — *"Any blood when you go to the toilet — bright red, or black and tarry? Is the stool pale, greasy or hard to flush?"*

**Why we ask:** Colour tells you the level of the bleed; pale, greasy, floating stool (steatorrhoea) points to fat malabsorption.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Melaena (black, tarry, offensive)** → upper GI bleed — pair with haematemesis question
- **Bright red coating the stool / on the paper** → anorectal (haemorrhoids, fissure)
- **Blood mixed through the stool + change in habit + weight loss** → colorectal cancer
- **Bloody diarrhoea with mucus** → colitis (IBD or infective)
- **Pale, greasy, floating, hard-to-flush stool (steatorrhoea)** → fat malabsorption (pancreatic insufficiency, biliary obstruction, coeliac)

---

### 7. Jaundice + urine/stool/itch — *"Any yellowing of eyes or skin? Dark urine or pale stool? Any itching?"*

**Why we ask:** The urine/stool/itch triad separates the types of jaundice at the bedside before any test.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Dark urine + pale stools + itch** → obstructive (post-hepatic) jaundice: gallstones or, if painless with weight loss, pancreatic head cancer (Courvoisier)
- **Normal urine/stool, ± anaemia** → pre-hepatic (haemolysis)
- **Jaundice + risk factors (alcohol, viral hepatitis, paracetamol, travel) + systemic upset** → hepatic

---

### 8. Appetite and weight — *"How is your appetite? Any weight loss?"*

**Why we ask:** Ties GI complaints to the constitutional malignancy screen; weight loss escalates the urgency of every symptom above.

---

## Discriminator table: upper vs lower GI bleed

| Clue | Upper GI bleed | Lower GI bleed |
|---|---|---|
| Vomiting | Haematemesis / coffee-ground | Absent |
| Stool | Melaena (black, tarry, offensive) | Fresh or dark red PR blood |
| Common causes | Peptic ulcer, varices, Mallory–Weiss | Diverticular, haemorrhoids, cancer, colitis, angiodysplasia |
| Ask about | NSAIDs, alcohol, liver disease | Change in bowel habit, weight loss |
| Caveat | — | A brisk upper GI bleed can present as fresh PR blood + shock |

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

**TOP:** dysphagia (solids→liquids?), heartburn/dyspepsia (ALARM?), nausea/vomiting (blood?), haematemesis · **MIDDLE:** abdominal pain (SOCRATES + site), appetite/weight · **BOTTOM:** change in bowel habit, PR bleeding/melaena, mucus · **OUTSIDE:** jaundice + dark urine/pale stool/itch
