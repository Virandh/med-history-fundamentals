# Skin (Dermatological) Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

For any skin complaint, characterise the lesion, then ask what it's doing and what it signals:

> **LESION → SYMPTOMS → CHANGE → SYSTEMIC → EXPOSURE**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Lesion** | The morphology | New rash/lesion — where, what does it look like, distribution |
| **Symptoms** | What it does | Itch, pain, weeping, bleeding |
| **Change** | Evolution | Changing size/shape/colour of a mole; non-healing |
| **Systemic** | Internal disease | Rash + fever/joint/mucosal involvement |
| **Exposure** | Triggers | New drugs, contacts, sun, occupation, travel |

Two red-flag threads: the **changing/non-healing lesion** (skin cancer / melanoma) and the **rash-plus-systemic-upset** (drug reaction, vasculitis, meningococcaemia, connective-tissue disease).

---

## The questions

### 1. The lesion / rash — *"Tell me about the rash — where did it start, where is it now, what does it look like?"*

**Why we ask:** Distribution and morphology are the whole diagnosis in dermatology. Extensor vs flexor, symmetrical vs localised, and dermatomal patterns each point somewhere specific.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How long have you had it, and has it changed since it started?"* — Duration and evolution — worsening, spreading, or resolving — narrows the timeline
- *"Where did it start, and where is it now — which parts of your skin are affected?"* — Site of onset and spread; sun-exposed vs clothing-contact areas point in different directions
- *"Was it flat, raised, or blistered to begin with?"* — The primary morphology (macule/papule/vesicle) is often more diagnostic than how it looks now, after scratching and evolution
- *"Is it itchy, and does anything make it better or worse?"* — Itch pattern and modifying factors help separate eczema, urticaria and contact causes
- *"Started any new tablets, creams, soaps or products in the last six weeks?"* — A new drug within days–weeks of a rash is a drug eruption until proven otherwise — patients often forget to mention antibiotics
- *"Any fever or joint pains with it?"* — Systemic upset alongside a rash changes the differential toward the dangerous — vasculitis, connective tissue disease, meningococcaemia
- *"Has anyone you live with or been in contact with got a similar rash?"* — Points to an infective or infestation cause (scabies, viral exanthem) shared by contacts
- *"Have you had this before?"* — If it's recurred in the same pattern, that itself supports a chronic/relapsing diagnosis rather than something new

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Symmetrical, extensor surfaces, well-demarcated scaly plaques** → psoriasis (ask about nails, joints, scalp)
- **Flexural, itchy, in an atopic person** → eczema/atopic dermatitis
- **Dermatomal, unilateral, painful vesicles not crossing the midline** → shingles (herpes zoster)
- **Target lesions** → erythema multiforme
- **A single well-defined patch matching contact with something** → contact dermatitis

### 2. Symptoms of the lesion — *"Is it itchy, painful, weeping or bleeding?"*

**Why we ask:** These qualities narrow the differential and gauge severity.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it itchy — and if so, is it worse at night?"* — Worse at night, especially with burrows between the fingers → scabies; daytime, diffuse → eczema or urticaria
- *"Is it painful, or more just uncomfortable?"* — Pain suggests an infective (cellulitis, zoster) or ischaemic process rather than a purely inflammatory rash
- **!** *"Is it weeping, crusting, or bleeding?"* — Bleeding, crusting or a non-healing quality is what should make you suspicious for skin malignancy rather than a benign inflammatory lesion
- *"Does anyone else in the house have the same itch?"* — Shared itch among household contacts points to an infestation like scabies

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Intensely itchy** → eczema, urticaria, scabies (worse at night, burrows, contacts affected), dermatitis herpetiformis
- **Painful** → infective (cellulitis, zoster) or ischaemic
- **Bleeding/crusting/non-healing** → suspicious for skin malignancy (see below)

---

### 3. Changing mole — *"Have any moles or spots changed in size, shape or colour?"*

**Why we ask:** Melanoma is caught by change — the ABCDE rule structures what to ask about.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Has it got bigger, or changed shape or colour?"* — Change in any of size, shape or colour is the trigger for concern, not any one feature alone
- *"Is it asymmetrical, with an irregular border, or more than one colour?"* — Runs through the ABCDE checklist — Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Colour variation, Diameter >6mm, Evolving
- *"Has it become itchy, bled, or crusted?"* — New symptoms in a pre-existing mole are as concerning as a visible change in appearance

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Changing mole — use ABCDE (Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Colour variation, Diameter >6 mm, Evolving)** → melanoma until excluded

**Red flag:** A new or changing pigmented lesion — never file under reassurance without looking.

---

### 4. Non-healing lesion — *"Anything on your skin that won't heal?"*

**Why we ask:** Keratinocyte cancers present as non-healing rather than as change — a lesion sitting there for months on sun-exposed skin is the tell.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How long has it been there without healing?"* — Any non-healing lesion beyond 4–6 weeks needs examination
- *"Is it pearly with visible tiny blood vessels, or more scaly and crusted?"* — Pearly nodule with telangiectasia and a rolled edge → basal cell carcinoma; scaly, crusted or ulcerated → squamous cell carcinoma
- *"Is it on a part of your skin that gets a lot of sun?"* — Sun-exposed sites are where keratinocyte cancers overwhelmingly arise

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Pearly nodule with telangiectasia / rolled edge, slowly growing, sun-exposed site** → basal cell carcinoma
- **Scaly/crusted or ulcerated non-healing lesion on sun-exposed skin** → squamous cell carcinoma
- **Any non-healing lesion >4–6 weeks** → needs examination

---

### 5. Systemic associations — *"Any fever, joint pains, mouth or genital ulcers, or feeling generally unwell with the rash?"*

**Why we ask:** Skin is a window on internal disease; a rash with systemic upset changes the whole differential toward the dangerous.

**If yes, ask:**
- **!** *"Does the rash blanch (go pale) when you press on it?"* — Non-blanching, with fever and feeling unwell → meningococcaemia/vasculitis, an emergency — do the glass/tumbler test
- **!** *"Any ulcers in your mouth or genitals, or is your skin peeling?"* — Mucosal involvement with skin peeling, especially after a new drug, → Stevens–Johnson syndrome/TEN, a dermatological emergency
- *"Any joint pains, hair loss, or sun sensitivity with the rash?"* — Photosensitive rash + joint pain + hair loss → SLE
- *"Any gut symptoms — diarrhoea or abdominal pain — alongside the joint and skin symptoms?"* — Rash + joint pain + gut symptoms sits in the connective-tissue/reactive spectrum

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Non-blanching purpuric rash + fever + unwell** → meningococcaemia / vasculitis — emergency red flag (do the glass/tumbler test)
- **Rash + mucosal involvement + skin peeling + new drug** → Stevens–Johnson / TEN — dermatological emergency
- **Photosensitive facial rash + joint pain + mouth ulcers + hair loss** → SLE
- **Rash + joint pain + gut symptoms** → the connective-tissue / reactive spectrum (cross-reference MSK)

**Red flag:** Non-blanching purpuric rash + fever is an emergency — do the glass test.

---

### 6. Exposure / triggers — *"Started any new medications, creams or products? Any new contacts, sun, occupation or travel?"*

**Why we ask:** So much of dermatology is external cause — drugs and contacts especially. A new drug within days–weeks of a rash is a drug eruption until proven otherwise.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Any new medications in the last six weeks, even over-the-counter ones?"* — New drug + widespread symmetrical rash → drug eruption; patients commonly forget short antibiotic courses
- *"Any new soaps, creams, cosmetics, or things you've touched at work or with a hobby?"* — A new topical or occupational contactant with a localised rash → contact dermatitis
- *"Has there been more sun exposure than usual, and is the rash on sun-exposed skin?"* — Sun exposure + a rash on exposed sites → photosensitivity, which can be idiopathic or drug-induced
- *"Any recent travel?"* — Travel-related infections and exposures can present with a rash days to weeks later

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **New drug + widespread symmetrical rash** → drug eruption (ask specifically — patients forget antibiotics)
- **New topical/occupational contactant + localised rash** → contact dermatitis
- **Sun exposure + rash on exposed sites** → photosensitivity (idiopathic or drug-induced)

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

LESION: site of onset & spread, morphology, distribution (extensor/flexor/dermatomal) · SYMPTOMS: itch, pain, weeping, bleeding · CHANGE: changing mole (ABCDE), non-healing lesion · SYSTEMIC: fever, joints, mucosal ulcers, unwell (non-blanching rash = emergency) · EXPOSURE: new drugs, contacts, sun, occupation, travel
