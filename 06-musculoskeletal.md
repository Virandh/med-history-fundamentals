# Musculoskeletal / Rheum Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

Ask what the joints/muscles are doing wrong, then whether it's systemic:

> **PAIN → STIFFNESS → SWELLING → FUNCTION → SYSTEMIC**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | Nociception | Joint/muscle/back pain — where, pattern |
| **Stiffness** | Inflammation vs mechanical | Morning stiffness + duration |
| **Swelling** | Synovitis | Swelling, warmth, redness |
| **Function** | Impact | Activities of daily living, grip, walking |
| **Systemic** | Connective tissue disease | Rash, eyes, Raynaud's, mouth ulcers, fatigue |

The master discriminator is **inflammatory vs mechanical**, and one question does most of the work: **duration of early-morning stiffness** (≥1 hour = classic RA / peripheral inflammatory; ≥30 min in the axial/spondyloarthritis context; <30 min or "after rest" = mechanical/OA). The pattern of joint involvement (which joints, symmetry, number) does the rest.

## The questions

### 1. Joint / limb pain — *"Which joints are painful? Sudden or gradual? Does it move around? Any injury?"*

**Why we ask:** The pattern — number of joints, symmetry, distribution, and tempo — is the core of rheumatological diagnosis.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Which joints are sore — one, or several? Can you point exactly where?"* — Monoarthritis vs oligo/polyarthritis, and the exact site vs referred pain, is the first branch point
- *"Is it symmetrical — the same joints on both sides — or one-sided, and are they small joints or large?"* — Symmetrical small joints (MCPs, PIPs, wrists) → RA · asymmetrical large weight-bearing joints → OA or seronegative disease
- *"Did it come on suddenly, or gradually — and any injury beforehand?"* — Sudden with no obvious cause suggests gout, infection, or reactive arthritis; gradual favours OA or RA
- **!** *"Any swelling, warmth, or redness around the joint?"* — Arthritis (visible inflammation) vs arthralgia (pain alone) — a single hot red joint needs urgent exclusion of septic arthritis
- *"Does activity make it better or worse, and what about cold weather?"* — Improves with activity → inflammatory; worse with activity, better with rest → mechanical
- *"How long has this been going on, and is it constant or does it come and go?"* — Constant/progressive vs episodic/migratory changes the differential — migratory large-joint pain suggests rheumatic fever, gonococcal, or reactive arthritis
- *"Any recent infection — a sore throat, gut bug, or new sexual partner?"* — Recent infection elsewhere raises reactive arthritis or, in a child, rheumatic fever
- *"Any change in your range of motion, or difficulty with daily tasks because of it?"* — Functional impact tracks severity better than pain alone and flags when to escalate

> Full work-up → **limb-joint-pain** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Symmetrical small joints (MCPs, PIPs, wrists), gradual** → rheumatoid arthritis
- **DIPs/PIPs and thumb base, often symmetrical (primary nodal OA — Heberden's/Bouchard's), or asymmetrical large weight-bearing joints (secondary OA), pain worse with use** → osteoarthritis
- **Single hot joint, acute, exquisite, first MTP** → gout (or, until excluded, septic arthritis — a red flag)
- **Migratory large-joint pain** → rheumatic fever, gonococcal, reactive
- **Axial (spine/sacroiliac) in a young adult with inflammatory pattern** → ankylosing spondylitis

**Red flag:** A single acutely hot, swollen, painful joint is septic arthritis until proven otherwise — needs aspiration, not reassurance.

### 2. Back pain — *"Any back pain? Anything make it worse? Does it go down your legs?"*

**Why we ask:** Mostly mechanical, but hosts several red-flag conditions; the pattern and associated features triage it.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Where exactly is the pain, and does it radiate anywhere — down a leg, or across to the front?"* — Radiation below the knee with dermatomal numbness/weakness → radiculopathy (disc); radiation from the abdomen/chest to the back raises a vascular cause (dissection)
- **!** *"Did it come on suddenly, or gradually?"* — Sudden with minimal or no trauma → think osteoporotic crush fracture, especially with risk factors (steroids, menopause, alcohol)
- *"Is it worse with activity and better with rest, or the opposite — worse at rest/at night, better with movement?"* — Worse with activity, better with rest, no systemic features → mechanical; worse at night/at rest, eases with exercise, in a young adult → inflammatory (spondyloarthritis)
- **!** *"Any numbness around your saddle area, or new problems controlling your bladder or bowel?"* — Saddle anaesthesia + sphincter disturbance → cauda equina, a surgical emergency
- **!** *"Any fever, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer?"* — Fever raises discitis/abscess (especially with IVDU); weight loss or a cancer history raises metastatic disease
- **!** *"How old are you, and is this a new problem?"* — New onset under 20 or over 55 is itself a red flag warranting further work-up rather than assuming mechanical back pain
- *"Any morning stiffness with it, and does exercise help?"* — Morning stiffness that eases with exercise, in a young adult, supports inflammatory back pain (spondyloarthritis) over a mechanical cause
- *"Any recent trauma, or known osteoporosis/long-term steroid use?"* — Combines with sudden onset to support a fragility fracture as the cause

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Worse with activity, better with rest, no systemic features** → mechanical
- **Inflammatory back pain: young adult, insidious, worse in the morning/at rest, better with exercise, night pain** → spondyloarthritis
- **Radiating below the knee + dermatomal numbness/weakness** → radiculopathy (disc)

**Red flag:** Bladder/bowel dysfunction + saddle anaesthesia (cauda equina), thoracic pain, fever/IVDU (discitis/abscess), weight loss/history of cancer (metastasis), age <20 or >55 new onset, trauma/osteoporosis (fracture).

### 3. Stiffness — *"Are your joints stiff in the morning? How long before they loosen?"*

**Why we ask:** The best single discriminator of inflammatory vs mechanical disease.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How long does the morning stiffness last before it eases?"* — ≥1 hour is classic for RA (≥30 min in axial spondyloarthritis); under 30 minutes points to a mechanical/OA pattern
- *"Does it ease as you get moving, or get worse through the day?"* — Eases with activity → inflammatory; worse with use through the day → mechanical
- *"Is it only after a period of sitting still, or is it there first thing regardless?"* — Stiffness specifically after inactivity (not necessarily overnight) is characteristic of OA of the hip or knee
- *"Which joints are affected — small hands and wrists, or hips and shoulders?"* — Small peripheral joints → RA pattern; proximal shoulder/hip girdle stiffness in someone over 50 → polymyalgia rheumatica
- **!** *"Are you over 50, and have you had any scalp tenderness, jaw pain chewing, or visual changes?"* — Proximal stiffness + age >50 raises polymyalgia rheumatica — screen for coexisting giant cell arteritis, which can cause permanent visual loss
- *"Is the stiffness widespread, with poor sleep and generalised pain, rather than joint-specific?"* — Widespread stiffness with non-refreshing sleep and diffuse pain, without deformity, suggests fibromyalgia rather than an inflammatory arthropathy

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Prolonged morning stiffness — ≥1 hour for classic RA (≥30 min in axial spondyloarthritis), easing with activity** → inflammatory (RA, spondyloarthritis). Note morning stiffness is a supportive clue, not a scored ACR/EULAR criterion
- **Brief stiffness (<30 min) or stiffness after rest/activity, worse through the day** → mechanical/OA
- **Proximal (shoulder/hip girdle) stiffness in someone >50 + raised inflammatory markers** → polymyalgia rheumatica (ask about GCA symptoms)

### 4. Swelling — *"Are the joints swollen? Warm or red?"*

**Why we ask:** Confirms synovitis (soft, boggy swelling) vs bony enlargement (OA) vs effusion, and warmth/redness raises infection/crystal disease.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is the swelling soft and boggy, or does it feel hard and bony?"* — Soft, boggy swelling → synovitis (inflammatory); hard bony swelling at the DIPs → Heberden's nodes (OA)
- *"Is it warm or red to look at?"* — Warmth and redness raise infection or crystal arthropathy over simple mechanical swelling
- **!** *"Is it one joint, or several — and did it come on suddenly?"* — A single hot, swollen joint of sudden onset is septic arthritis until excluded — needs aspiration, not reassurance
- *"Does the swelling come and go, or is it persistent?"* — Persistent, symmetrical small-joint swelling supports RA; episodic swelling around a single joint supports gout or pseudogout

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Soft symmetrical swelling of small joints** → inflammatory synovitis (RA)
- **Hard, bony swellings at DIPs (Heberden's)** → OA
- **Single hot red swollen joint** → gout/pseudogout/sepsis

---

### 5. Functional impact (ADLs) — *"What can't you do now that you could before — dressing, gripping, stairs?"*

**Why we ask:** Rheumatology is a functional specialty; disability drives management and reveals severity better than pain scores.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"What specifically can't you manage now — dressing, gripping a cup, stairs, opening jars?"* — Concrete functional examples are more reliable and specific than a global severity rating
- *"Has this been getting steadily worse, or does it fluctuate with flares?"* — Steady decline suggests accumulating joint damage; fluctuation with flares tracks disease activity instead

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Difficulty with fine tasks (buttons, jar lids, gripping)** → hand involvement affecting function, independent of pain severity
- **Needing walking aids or help with self-care** → higher-grade functional impairment, guiding escalation of treatment

---

### 6. Deformity / instability — *"Any visible deformity, or joints giving way / feeling unstable?"*

**Why we ask:** Deformity and instability signal structural joint damage — chronic erosive disease or ligamentous injury — rather than active inflammation alone.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Has the shape of the joint changed gradually, or did it happen suddenly, like after an injury?"* — Progressive change suggests chronic erosive joint damage; sudden change after trauma suggests dislocation or ligament injury
- *"Does it feel like it "gives way" or comes out of place?"* — Giving way suggests instability from ligamentous laxity or muscle weakness rather than active inflammation alone

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Progressive deformity (e.g. ulnar deviation, swan-neck fingers)** → chronic erosive joint damage from longstanding inflammatory arthritis
- **Joint "giving way" or dislocating** → ligamentous instability or muscle weakness, a mechanical rather than inflammatory problem

---

### 7. Skin, mucosal & eye screen — *"Any skin rashes, mouth ulcers, or dry/red eyes?"*

**Why we ask:** Extra-articular skin and mucosal clues point to a specific connective tissue disease and separate it from isolated joint disease.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Any rash that comes up in the sun, or mouth ulcers?"* — Photosensitive rash + mouth ulcers + arthralgia → SLE
- *"Any psoriasis — on your skin, scalp, or nails?"* — Psoriasis + arthritis → psoriatic arthritis, and joint disease can precede the rash
- *"Any diarrhoea, blood in the stool, or red/painful eyes alongside the joint symptoms?"* — Arthritis + GI or eye inflammation → the seronegative/reactive/enteropathic spectrum

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Photosensitive malar rash + mouth ulcers + arthralgia** → SLE
- **Psoriasis (skin/nails) + arthritis** → psoriatic arthritis
- **Dry eyes + dry mouth** → Sjögren's
- **Arthritis + diarrhoea/bloody stool or eye inflammation** → enteropathic/reactive (the seronegative spectrum)

---

### 8. Raynaud's, hair loss & fatigue — *"Do your fingers change colour in the cold? Any hair loss or persistent fatigue?"*

**Why we ask:** Raynaud's (white→blue→red) with rash/arthralgia points to SLE, systemic sclerosis or mixed CTD; hair loss and fatigue are non-specific but common accompaniments of active CTD.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Do your fingers or toes go white, then blue, then red in the cold?"* — The triphasic colour change is what distinguishes true Raynaud's from simply feeling cold
- *"Is it just your fingers, or do you get skin tightening, difficulty swallowing, or breathlessness too?"* — Additional features alongside Raynaud's raise systemic sclerosis or another connective tissue disease rather than primary (benign) Raynaud's
- *"Any patchy hair loss, or fatigue that's more than just being busy or tired?"* — Non-specific alone, but a common accompaniment of active connective tissue disease when combined with the other features

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Raynaud's (white** →blue→red fingers in cold) + rash/arthralgia → SLE, systemic sclerosis, mixed CTD

## Discriminator table: inflammatory vs mechanical joint/back pain

| Clue | Inflammatory | Mechanical / degenerative |
|---|---|---|
| Morning stiffness | ≥30–60 min (≥1 hr classic RA) | <30 min, brief |
| Effect of activity | Eases with use | Worse with use |
| Effect of rest | Worse (stiffens up) | Better |
| Night pain | Common, wakes them | Uncommon |
| Swelling | Soft, warm synovitis | Bony, cool |
| Systemic features | Fatigue, rash, eyes, weight loss | Absent |

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

PAIN: which joints, pattern (symmetry/number/distribution), tempo, back pain (+ red flags) · STIFFNESS: morning stiffness duration (inflammatory vs mechanical) · SWELLING: swelling/warmth/redness (single hot joint = ?septic) · FUNCTION: ADLs, grip, mobility · SYSTEMIC: rash, eyes, mouth ulcers, Raynaud's, hair loss, fatigue
