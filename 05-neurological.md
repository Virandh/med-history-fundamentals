# Neurological Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

Work from the top of the neuraxis down, then out to the special senses:

> **BRAIN → CRANIAL → CORD/NERVE → EPISODES → HEAD**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Brain** | Cortex / global | Confusion, memory, speech |
| **Cranial** | Cranial nerves | Vision, hearing, swallowing, facial weakness, smell |
| **Cord/nerve** | Motor & sensory tracts | Weakness, numbness/tingling, coordination/balance, gait |
| **Episodes** | Paroxysmal | Blackouts, seizures, dizziness/vertigo |
| **Head** | Meninges/vessels | Headache |

The single most useful discriminator running through the whole system is the tempo of onset: sudden (seconds–minutes) → vascular (stroke, haemorrhage, SAH); subacute (days–weeks) → inflammatory/infective/compressive; chronic (months–years) → degenerative/neoplastic.

> **See also** — Psychiatric — cognition, delirium and dementia overlap; ENT & Eyes — vision and vertigo

## The questions

### 1. Confusion / delirium — *"Has anyone noticed you seem more confused than usual — coming and going, or constant?"*

**Why we ask:** Fluctuating confusion over hours–days is delirium until an underlying systemic cause is found; a gradual, non-fluctuating decline points instead to dementia.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Acute confusion over hours–days, fluctuating, ± reversed sleep–wake** → delirium — look for the systemic cause (infection, drugs, metabolic)

---

### 2. Memory — *"Any problems with your memory?"*

**Why we ask:** Progressive memory loss with functional decline in an older adult raises dementia; get a collateral history, since patients often underreport it.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Progressive memory loss + functional decline in the elderly** → dementia (get collateral history)

---

### 3. Speech — *"Any trouble finding words, or getting words out clearly?"*

**Why we ask:** Dysphasia (word-finding/language) localises to the dominant hemisphere; dysarthria (slurred articulation) localises to the cerebellum, bulbar muscles or their nerve supply — the distinction matters for localisation.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Sudden word-finding difficulty or slurred speech** → stroke/TIA until excluded
- **Gradual word-finding difficulty in an older adult** → consider primary progressive aphasia / early dementia

---

### 4. Vision (neuro screen) — *"Any change in your vision, or a curtain/shadow across it?"*

**Why we ask:** Sudden painless monocular loss in the amaurosis fugax pattern is a TIA warning — cross-reference the ENT & Eyes visual-loss entry for the full differential.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Sudden painless monocular visual loss ("curtain")** → amaurosis fugax / retinal artery occlusion — a TIA warning

---

### 5. Hearing (neuro screen) — *"Any change in your hearing?"*

**Why we ask:** New unilateral hearing loss with other cranial nerve signs raises a cerebellopontine angle lesion — cross-reference the ENT & Eyes hearing-loss entry.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Unilateral hearing loss + facial numbness/weakness or ataxia** → consider a cerebellopontine angle lesion (e.g. acoustic neuroma)

---

### 6. Smell — *"Any change in your sense of smell?"*

**Why we ask:** Isolated anosmia can be an early non-motor feature of Parkinson's disease, or reflect a frontal skull-base lesion pressing on the olfactory tract.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Isolated anosmia, older adult, no nasal cause** → consider a neurodegenerative cause

---

### 7. Facial weakness — *"Any weakness or drooping of the face?"*

**Why we ask:** Sparing of the forehead localises an upper motor neuron (central, e.g. stroke) lesion; forehead involvement means a lower motor neuron (peripheral) lesion — the single most useful localiser in facial weakness.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Forehead spared, lower face only** → UMN (stroke) — usually with other focal signs
- **Whole side of the face including the forehead** → LMN (Bell's palsy; exclude Ramsay Hunt or a parotid tumour)

**Red flag:** Sudden facial weakness with other focal neuro signs → stroke until excluded.

---

### 8. Diplopia (neuro screen) — *"Any double vision?"*

**Why we ask:** Diplopia localises a cranial nerve palsy (III, IV, VI) — think microvascular, compressive (aneurysm), or raised ICP.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **** → cranial nerve palsy (III, IV, VI) — microvascular, compressive (aneurysm), or raised ICP

**Red flag:** Painful third nerve palsy with a fixed dilated pupil → compressive aneurysm, a neurosurgical emergency.

---

### 9. Weakness — *"Any weakness in the arms or legs — one side or both? Sudden or gradual?"*

**Why we ask:** Distribution + tempo localise the lesion along the neuraxis.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Sudden, one-sided (face/arm/leg) ± speech** → stroke/TIA — a time-critical red flag
- **Ascending symmetrical weakness over days ± after infection** → Guillain–Barré
- **Proximal, symmetrical, gradual (difficulty with stairs/rising/hair)** → myopathy
- **Weakness worse with use, better with rest, ± ptosis/diplopia** → myasthenia

**Red flag:** Weakness + a sensory level + bladder involvement → cord compression.

---

### 10. Sensory disturbance — *"Any numbness, pins and needles or altered sensation? Where?"*

**Why we ask:** The distribution maps the lesion (nerve, root, cord, or brain).

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Glove-and-stocking** → peripheral neuropathy (diabetes, alcohol, B12)
- **Single nerve territory** → mononeuropathy/entrapment (e.g. median → carpal tunnel)
- **Dermatomal** → nerve root (disc)
- **A sensory level on the trunk** → spinal cord lesion — red flag
- **One-sided, sudden, with weakness** → stroke

---

### 11. Coordination / gait / falls — *"Any problems with balance, coordination, or walking? Any falls?"*

**Why we ask:** Screens the cerebellum, proprioception and the motor system as an integrated output; falls are a major morbidity in the elderly.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Broad-based, clumsy, worse in the dark improving with vision** → sensory ataxia (proprioceptive)
- **Broad-based with slurred speech/tremor, not helped by vision** → cerebellar
- **Shuffling, small steps, stooped, reduced arm swing** → parkinsonism
- **High-stepping (foot drop)** → peripheral nerve

---

### 12. Sphincter / continence — *"Any new problems with bladder or bowel control?"*

**Why we ask:** New sphincter disturbance with leg symptoms is a spinal-cord red flag — a small question that catches a surgical emergency.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Sphincter disturbance (new urinary retention/incontinence or bowel incontinence) + bilateral leg symptoms + saddle numbness** → cauda equina / cord compression
- **Isolated urge/stress incontinence** → see GU (usually not neurological)

**Red flag:** Sphincter disturbance + saddle anaesthesia + bilateral leg weakness → cauda equina — urgent.

---

### 13. Blackouts — *"Have you had any episodes of losing consciousness?"*

**Why we ask:** The overlap of syncope (cardiovascular) and seizure (neurological) — the history distinguishes them (cross-reference the cardio syncope entry).

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Aura, lateral tongue-bite, post-ictal confusion/drowsiness** → seizure (jerk duration doesn't discriminate: brief clonic jerks occur in convulsive syncope, and prolonged jerking suggests non-epileptic seizures; incontinence and tongue-biting alone are of limited value)
- **Prodromal nausea/greying vision, triggered by standing/pain, rapid clear recovery** → vasovagal syncope
- **Exertional, no warning** → cardiac (see cardio file) — the dangerous one
- Always seek a collateral history from a witness — the patient by definition didn't see it

---

### 14. Seizures — *"Have you ever had a fit or convulsion?"*

**Why we ask:** Distinguishes provoked from unprovoked and localises. Ask about aura (focal onset), and precipitants (alcohol withdrawal, sleep deprivation, flashing lights, missed medication).

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Preceding aura** → focal onset
- Precipitants: alcohol withdrawal, sleep deprivation, missed medication

---

### 15. Dizziness / vertigo — *"When you say dizzy, do you mean the room spins, or more light-headed?"*

**Why we ask:** "Dizziness" splits into two unrelated pathways — force the patient to define it.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Room spinning (vertigo)** → vestibular: BPPV (brief, positional), vestibular neuritis (acute, sustained), Ménière's (with hearing loss/tinnitus); central causes (brainstem/cerebellar stroke) if with other neuro signs → red flag
- **Light-headed/faint** → presyncope: cardiovascular/orthostatic (back to the cardio pathway)
- **Unsteady only when walking** → gait/proprioceptive/cerebellar

---

### 16. Headache — *"Do you get headaches? Tell me about them."*

**Why we ask:** Overwhelmingly benign (tension, migraine) but hosts several neurological emergencies — the whole skill is pattern-matching the dangerous ones.

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Thunderclap — instant, worst-ever, "hit on the head"** → subarachnoid haemorrhage until excluded
- **Headache + fever + neck stiffness + photophobia ± rash** → meningitis
- **Worse in the morning / on coughing/bending, with vomiting** → raised intracranial pressure (mass)
- **New headache >50 with scalp tenderness, jaw claudication, visual loss** → giant cell arteritis
- **Unilateral, throbbing, with aura/nausea/photophobia, recurrent** → migraine
- **Band-like, bilateral, stress-related** → tension-type

**Red flag:** SNOOP: Systemic (fever, weight loss), Neuro deficit, Onset sudden, Older (>50 new), Papilloedema/positional/pattern change.

## Discriminator table: peripheral vs central vertigo

| Clue | Peripheral (vestibular) | Central (brainstem/cerebellar) |
|---|---|---|
| Hearing loss / tinnitus | Common | Usually absent |
| Other neuro signs | Absent | Present (diplopia, dysarthria, ataxia, weakness) |
| Nystagmus | Horizontal, fatigable, suppressed by fixation | Vertical / direction-changing, not suppressed |
| Gait | Unsteady but can walk | Often cannot stand or walk |
| Examples | BPPV, vestibular neuritis, Ménière’s | Stroke / TIA — red flag |

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

BRAIN: confusion/delirium, memory, speech · CRANIAL: vision, hearing, smell, facial weakness, diplopia · CORD/NERVE: weakness (sudden vs gradual), numbness/tingling (distribution), coordination/balance/gait, falls · EPISODES: blackouts, seizures, dizziness (vertigo vs faint) · HEAD: headache (SOCRATES + SNOOP red flags)
