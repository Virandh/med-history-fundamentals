# Genitourinary / Renal Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

Follow the urine from kidney to outside, splitting the lower-tract symptoms into the two classic groups, then finish on renal failure:

> **STORAGE → VOIDING → CONTENT → RENAL FAILURE**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Storage (irritative)** | Bladder filling | Frequency, urgency, nocturia, incontinence |
| **Voiding (obstructive)** | Bladder emptying / outflow | Hesitancy, poor stream, dribbling, pis-en-deux, retention |
| **Content** | What's in the urine | Dysuria, haematuria, appearance, loin pain |
| **Renal failure** | Failing kidneys | Oedema, itch, bruising, hiccups |

The **storage (irritative) vs voiding (obstructive)** split is the key LUTS framework: storage symptoms point to the bladder itself (infection, overactivity), voiding symptoms point to outflow obstruction (prostate). Haematuria is the red-flag thread throughout.

> **See also** — Reproductive & Sexual History — menstrual, pelvic, testicular, STI questions (the two overlap at dysuria and discharge)

## The questions

### 1. Storage / irritative symptoms — *"How often do you pass urine? Do you have to rush? Up at night — how many times?"*

**Why we ask:** Frequency, urgency and nocturia together define an irritable/overactive bladder or infection.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How many times a day, and how many times overnight?"* — Quantify frequency and nocturia — the baseline to track change and severity
- *"Do you get a sudden strong urge that's hard to hold on, or is it more just going often?"* — Urgency implies detrusor overactivity/overactive bladder rather than simple frequency
- *"Any burning or stinging when you go, or cloudy/smelly urine?"* — Dysuria + cloudy urine → UTI
- *"Are you drinking more, or losing weight without trying?"* — Polyuria + polydipsia + weight loss → screen for diabetes
- *"Any breathlessness, ankle swelling, or known heart failure?"* — Nocturia alone can be nocturnal fluid redistribution from cardiac failure, not a bladder problem
- *"In men — any trouble starting, weak stream, or a feeling of not emptying alongside this?"* — Combined storage + voiding symptoms in an older man points to prostatic outflow obstruction
- **!** *"Any fever, loin pain, or feeling generally unwell?"* — Suggests upper tract involvement (pyelonephritis) rather than simple cystitis

> Full work-up → **micturition** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **+ dysuria + cloudy urine** → UTI
- **+ polyuria + polydipsia + weight loss** → screen for diabetes
- **Nocturia alone** → also cardiac failure (nocturnal fluid redistribution) and, in older men, prostatic disease
- **Fever + loin pain alongside storage symptoms** → upper tract involvement (pyelonephritis), not simple cystitis

### 2. Incontinence — *"Do you ever leak urine? With coughing/laughing, or with a sudden urge you can’t defer?"*

**Why we ask:** The trigger separates two mechanisms with opposite treatments.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Does it leak with coughing, laughing, sneezing or lifting, or with a sudden urge you can't defer?"* — Leak with raised intra-abdominal pressure → stress incontinence; leak preceded by urge → urge incontinence (overactive bladder) — opposite mechanisms, opposite treatments
- *"Is it a small squirt, or does it empty the whole bladder?"* — Small squirts → stress · larger volume, may empty the bladder → urge
- *"Is it more a continuous dribble, especially after you think you've finished?"* — Continuous dribble suggests overflow (chronic retention) or, rarely, a fistula — a different mechanism again
- **!** *"Any back pain, numbness around the saddle area, or weakness/numbness in the legs with it?"* — New incontinence + back pain + saddle anaesthesia + bilateral leg symptoms → cauda equina, a surgical emergency
- *"Did this start after a delivery, around menopause, or with a new medical problem or medication?"* — Pelvic floor trauma and oestrogen withdrawal point to stress incontinence; a new neurological trigger or medication points elsewhere

> Full work-up → **micturition** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Leak on coughing/laughing/lifting** → stress incontinence (pelvic floor)
- **Leak preceded by a sudden unstoppable urge** → urge incontinence (overactive bladder)
- **Continuous dribble** → overflow (chronic retention) or fistula

**Red flag:** New incontinence + back pain + saddle anaesthesia + bilateral leg symptoms → cauda equina.

---

### 3. Voiding / obstructive symptoms — *"Trouble starting? Weak stream? Dribble at the end, feel you haven’t emptied, or need to go again straight away?"*

**Why we ask:** Hesitancy, poor stream, terminal dribbling, pis-en-deux (double voiding — passing urine, then needing to go again minutes later) and incomplete emptying are the classic outflow obstruction cluster — in older men that means the prostate.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it hard to start, and does the stream come out weak or interrupted?"* — Hesitancy + poor/intermittent stream are the earliest outflow-obstruction symptoms
- *"Does it dribble at the end, and do you feel you've fully emptied?"* — Terminal dribbling and a sense of incomplete emptying complete the classic BPH picture
- *"Do you need to go again just minutes after you've just been?"* — Pis-en-deux (double voiding) is a specific marker of incomplete emptying from outflow obstruction
- **!** *"Are you completely unable to pass urine, with a painful, swollen lower tummy?"* — Complete retention with a painful distended bladder is acute urinary retention — an emergency
- **!** *"Any blood in the urine, bone pain, or weight loss alongside this?"* — In an older man these symptoms should prompt excluding prostate cancer, not just assuming benign hypertrophy
- *"Is the dribbling painless and continuous, without you noticing the urge?"* — Painless overflow incontinence without sensation suggests chronic retention or a neurogenic bladder rather than simple BPH
- *"Any fevers or recurrent infections alongside these symptoms?"* — Obstruction predisposes to urinary infection — treat the obstruction, not just the infection, or it will recur

> Full work-up → **micturition** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Voiding LUTS in an older man** → BPH; with weight loss/bone pain/haematuria → exclude prostate cancer
- **Complete inability to pass urine + painful distended bladder** → acute urinary retention (emergency)
- **Painless retention with overflow incontinence** → chronic retention/neurogenic bladder

**Red flag:** Acute urinary retention is an emergency.

---

### 4. Dysuria — *"Any pain or burning when you pass urine?"*

**Why we ask:** The cardinal symptom of lower urinary tract infection/inflammation.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Any need to go more often, or urgently, with it?"* — Dysuria + frequency + urgency + suprapubic pain → cystitis/UTI
- **!** *"Any fever, chills/rigors, or pain in your back or side?"* — Fever + loin pain + rigors → pyelonephritis, the more serious upper-tract infection
- *"Any discharge from the urethra or vagina, and are you sexually active?"* — Discharge + a relevant sexual history → consider an STI rather than a straightforward UTI
- *"Is the burning at the start of your stream, or throughout/at the end?"* — Burning right at the start, clearing after, points more to a urethral cause; burning throughout suggests cystitis

> Full work-up → **micturition** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **+ frequency + urgency + suprapubic pain** → cystitis/UTI
- **+ fever + loin pain + rigors** → pyelonephritis (upper tract — more serious)
- **+ urethral/vaginal discharge + sexual history** → STI (cross-reference the reproductive history)

---

### 5. Haematuria — *"Have you ever seen blood in your urine?"*

**Why we ask:** The GU red flag. Any visible haematuria needs an explanation — the differential includes urological cancers.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Was the blood visible to you, or was this picked up on a test?"* — Visible (macroscopic) haematuria carries a much higher cancer risk than an incidental finding of microscopic blood — investigate both, but with different urgency
- **!** *"Is it painful, or completely painless?"* — Painless visible haematuria → bladder/renal cancer until excluded, especially in an older smoker
- *"Any colicky loin-to-groin pain with it?"* — Colicky pain alongside blood points to a ureteric stone rather than malignancy
- *"Any burning, frequency, or fever with it?"* — Suggests a UTI as the source rather than a structural or malignant cause
- *"Did this start within a few days of a sore throat, or 1–2 weeks after a throat or skin infection?"* — Days after a sore throat (synpharyngitic) → IgA nephropathy · 1–2 weeks after, with hypertension/oedema, → post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
- *"Could this be your period, something you've eaten like beetroot, or a new medication like rifampicin?"* — Rules out the mimics before committing to a true haematuria work-up
- **!** *"Do you smoke, and have you lost weight?"* — The classic risk profile for urothelial and renal malignancy
- *"Are you on blood thinners, or have you had a recent catheter, procedure, or injury?"* — Anticoagulation and instrumentation are common, less sinister explanations — but painless haematuria should still be investigated even on anticoagulants

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Painless visible haematuria** → bladder/renal cancer until excluded (esp. older, smoker)
- **+ loin-to-groin colic** → stone
- **+ dysuria/frequency/fever** → UTI
- **Within a few days of a sore throat (synpharyngitic)** → IgA nephropathy; ~1–2 weeks after a throat/skin infection, with hypertension/oedema → post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
- Confirm it isn't menstrual, dietary (beetroot), or drug-related (rifampicin) discolouration

**Red flag:** Painless visible haematuria is cancer until excluded.

---

### 6. Urine appearance — *"Any change in the colour, smell, or how frothy your urine is? How much are you passing?"*

**Why we ask:** Frothiness and smell add renal clues beyond blood alone.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it frothy, tea-coloured, or does it have an unusual smell?"* — Persistent froth → significant proteinuria (nephrotic range); offensive smell → infection
- *"How much are you passing overall — more than usual, or noticeably less?"* — Very little (oliguria/anuria) raises acute kidney injury; unusually large volumes raise diabetes or a concentrating defect

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Frothy urine** → significant proteinuria (nephrotic range); offensive smell → infection

---

### 7. Loin pain — *"Any pain in your back or side, below the ribs?"*

**Why we ask:** Localises disease to the kidney/ureter.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it colicky — coming in waves — or a constant ache?"* — Colicky, severe, patient writhing → ureteric stone; constant → infection or obstruction
- *"Does it radiate anywhere — down towards the groin or testis?"* — Loin-to-groin radiation is classic for a ureteric stone tracking down the ureter
- **!** *"Any fever, rigors, or feeling generally unwell with it?"* — Constant loin pain + fever + rigors → pyelonephritis
- *"Is it on one side, or a dull ache on both sides?"* — Dull bilateral loin ache raises obstruction or polycystic kidneys rather than a single stone
- *"Any family history of kidney problems, or high blood pressure?"* — Family history of enlarged kidneys and hypertension → polycystic kidney disease

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Severe colicky loin-to-groin, patient writhing** → ureteric stone
- **Constant loin pain + fever + rigors** → pyelonephritis
- **Dull bilateral loin ache** → obstruction, polycystic kidneys

---

### 8. Oedema (renal) — *"Any swelling, especially around the eyes or ankles?"*

**Why we ask:** Fluid retention from a failing kidney has a nephrotic (heavy proteinuria, periorbital) or nephritic (salt/water retention) pattern.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Is it around your eyes in the morning, or more in your ankles by evening?"* — Periorbital swelling suggests a nephrotic (heavy proteinuria) pattern; ankle swelling worse by evening is a more generalised fluid-retention pattern
- *"Is your urine frothy, and have you noticed weight gain?"* — Oedema + frothy urine → a nephrotic/nephritic picture rather than a cardiac or hepatic cause

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Oedema + frothy urine** → nephrotic/nephritic picture

---

### 9. Uraemia screen — *"Any generalised itching, easy bruising, or persistent hiccups?"*

**Why we ask:** As the kidneys fail, retained toxins produce a quiet cluster that's easy to miss unless you ask for it specifically.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Any nausea, poor appetite, or unusual tiredness alongside this?"* — Completes the uraemic cluster — itch, bruising, hiccups, nausea and fatigue together suggest advanced renal failure
- *"How much urine are you passing overall?"* — A marked reduction (oliguria/anuria) points to acute kidney injury — look for a pre-renal, renal, or post-renal cause
- *"Do you know your kidney function results — creatinine or eGFR?"* — Many patients already know this number and it tells you how advanced the picture is before you order anything

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Generalised itch (pruritus) + easy bruising + hiccups + nausea + fatigue** → uraemia (advanced renal failure)
- **Reduced urine output (oliguria/anuria)** → acute kidney injury — look for the cause (pre-renal, renal, post-renal)

## Discriminator table: stress vs urge incontinence

| Clue | Stress | Urge |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Cough, laugh, sneeze, lift (raised intra-abdominal pressure) | Sudden compelling urge, can’t defer |
| Volume leaked | Small squirts | Larger; may empty the bladder |
| Nocturia / enuresis | Uncommon | Common |
| Mechanism | Pelvic floor / sphincter weakness | Detrusor overactivity |
| Typical patient | Post-vaginal-delivery, post-menopausal | Any; neurological disease, bladder irritation |

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

**STORAGE:** frequency, urgency, nocturia, incontinence (stress vs urge) · **VOIDING:** hesitancy, poor stream, dribbling, pis-en-deux, retention · **CONTENT:** dysuria, haematuria (painless = red flag), appearance (frothy/offensive), volume, loin pain · **RENAL FAILURE:** oedema, itch, bruising, hiccups · **plus** the Reproductive & Sexual History screen alongside
