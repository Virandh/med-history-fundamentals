# General / Constitutional Systems Review

## The scaffold (how to reconstruct this list under pressure)

The constitutional screen is the body's "check engine light" — non-specific signals that something systemic (malignancy, infection, inflammation, endocrine) is going on. Group them by what they signal:

> **WASTING → THERMOSTAT → ENERGY → APPETITE → LUMPS**

| Chunk | What it probes | Questions it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Wasting** | Catabolic/malignant/chronic disease | Weight change, appetite |
| **Thermostat** | Infection, inflammation, malignancy | Fever, night sweats, rigors |
| **Energy** | Almost everything | Fatigue, malaise, sleep |
| **Appetite** | GI, malignancy, depression | Appetite, intake |
| **Lumps** | Lymphoreticular, malignancy | Lumps/bumps, itch |

This is the screen that catches the "must-not-miss" systemic diseases the organ-specific screens can miss. The **B-symptom triad** (weight loss + fever + drenching night sweats) lives here and is one of the highest-yield clusters in the systems review — it flags lymphoma and TB.

## The questions

### 1. Weight change — *"Has your weight changed recently without trying? Are your clothes looser?"*

**Why we ask:** Unintentional weight loss is one of the most important symptoms in medicine. Quantify it (kilograms, timeframe) and confirm it's unintentional. >5% body weight over 6–12 months is significant.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How much weight, and over what timeframe?"* — Quantify — over 5% of body weight over 6–12 months, unintentional, is what makes this significant
- *"Is it definitely unintentional — no change in diet, activity, or dieting?"* — Confirms it isn't explained by a deliberate change before chasing an organic cause
- *"Has your appetite gone up, stayed the same, or gone down?"* — Weight loss + good/increased appetite → hypermetabolic (hyperthyroidism, uncontrolled diabetes) · weight loss + poor appetite → malignancy, chronic infection, depression, organ failure
- **!** *"Any fevers, night sweats, or lumps alongside the weight change?"* — Completes the B-symptom triad and raises lymphoma or TB
- *"Any change in bowel habit, or blood loss from anywhere?"* — Links the weight change to the GI malignancy and anaemia screens
- *"Any symptoms of an overactive or underactive thyroid — heat/cold intolerance, palpitations, bowel changes?"* — Thyroid disease is one of the most treatable, easily missed causes of unexplained weight change in either direction
- *"Any swelling of your legs or abdomen?"* — Rapid weight gain over days is fluid retention, not fat — think cardiac, renal, or liver failure
- *"How are you feeling in yourself — mood, energy, sleep?"* — Depression can present as weight change with reduced appetite and deserves equal billing with the organic causes

> Full work-up → **weight-loss** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Unintentional weight loss + good/increased appetite** → hypermetabolic: hyperthyroidism, or uncontrolled diabetes (osmotic loss)
- **Unintentional weight loss + poor appetite** → malignancy, chronic infection (TB, HIV), depression, chronic organ failure
- **Weight gain** → fluid (cardiac/renal/liver failure), hypothyroidism, Cushing's, or simple positive energy balance
- **Rapid weight gain over days** → fluid retention, not fat

**Red flag:** unexplained weight loss is a cancer red flag at any age — pair it with the sinister screens of every organ system.

### 2. Fever, rigors, night sweats — *"Any fevers or shivering attacks? Drenching night sweats where you change the sheets?"*

**Why we ask:** Fever signals infection/inflammation/malignancy. **Rigors** (uncontrollable shaking) suggest bacteraemia. **Drenching** night sweats flag TB, lymphoma, and endocarditis.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Do you get uncontrollable shaking, or just feeling hot?"* — True rigors (shaking chills) suggest bacteraemia, not just a low-grade temperature
- *"Are the night sweats drenching enough to need changing the sheets?"* — Drenching, not just feeling warm at night, is the specific pattern that flags TB, lymphoma and endocarditis
- *"Any symptoms pointing to where the infection might be — cough, urinary symptoms, abdominal pain?"* — Rigors with localising symptoms points to a specific bacterial source — pyelonephritis, cholangitis, pneumonia, an abscess
- **!** *"Any weight loss or lumps alongside it?"* — Completes the B-symptom triad and raises lymphoma
- *"Any heart murmur, IV drug use, or recent dental work?"* — Recurrent fevers with any of these raises infective endocarditis
- *"Any recent travel, and if so, where?"* — Cyclical fevers with travel to an endemic area raise malaria — ask specifically about the pattern and timing
- *"How long has this been going on, and is there a pattern — continuous, or coming and going?"* — Duration and pattern (continuous vs relapsing vs cyclical) narrow the infective and inflammatory differential
- *"Any joint pains, rash, or mouth ulcers with the fever?"* — Raises an inflammatory/connective tissue cause rather than a purely infective one

> Full work-up → **fever** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Rigors + localising symptoms** → bacterial infection (pyelonephritis, cholangitis, pneumonia, abscess)
- **Drenching night sweats + weight loss + lumps** → lymphoma; + cough/travel → TB
- **Recurrent fevers with a heart murmur / IVDU / dental work** → endocarditis
- **Cyclical fevers with travel to endemic area** → malaria

### 3. Fatigue / malaise — *"How is your energy overall? Do you feel generally run down?"*

**Why we ask:** The most common and least specific symptom — its value is contextual. Screen for the treatable drivers rather than accepting "tired" at face value.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"How is your sleep — enough hours, and does it feel refreshing?"* — Poor sleep quantity or quality is one of the commonest, most under-asked explanations for fatigue — check before attributing it to disease
- *"Any pallor, breathlessness, or dizziness alongside it?"* — Fatigue + pallor + breathlessness → anaemia
- *"Any cold intolerance, weight gain, or constipation?"* — Fatigue + these → hypothyroidism
- *"How's your mood — low, lost interest in things, sleep or appetite changed?"* — Fatigue + low mood + anhedonia → depression, and deserves equal weight to the organic causes
- *"Any thirst or passing more urine than usual?"* — Fatigue + polyuria/polydipsia → diabetes
- *"What medications are you on — any sedatives, beta-blockers, or antidepressants?"* — A surprisingly common and easily reversible cause — always check before an extensive work-up
- *"How much alcohol do you drink, and what's your activity level like?"* — Lifestyle factors (alcohol, deconditioning, shift work) are common contributors that are easy to overlook
- *"Is it getting worse with any activity — physical, mental, or emotional — and not improved by rest?"* — Post-exertional malaise unimproved by rest, lasting more than 6 months, points towards chronic fatigue syndrome rather than a single reversible cause

> Full work-up → **tiredness** approach

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Fatigue + pallor + breathlessness** → anaemia
- **Fatigue + cold intolerance + weight gain + constipation** → hypothyroidism
- **Fatigue + low mood + anhedonia + sleep/appetite change** → depression
- **Fatigue + polyuria/polydipsia** → diabetes
- Always ask about sleep quantity and quality and medications (beta-blockers, sedatives) before attributing to disease

### 4. Appetite — *"How is your appetite? Are you eating normally?"*

**Why we ask:** Appetite links the constitutional screen to GI, endocrine and psychiatric systems, and modifies the interpretation of weight change (see above).

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Has it gone up, down, or stayed the same?"* — Direction of change, paired with weight change, narrows the differential — see the weight-change question
- *"Is there a reason you might be eating less — nausea, pain after eating, low mood, or difficulty swallowing?"* — Screens the mechanical and psychological explanations before assuming a systemic cause
- *"How long has this been going on?"* — A longstanding change is more likely functional/chronic; a recent, clear-cut change deserves more urgent attention
- *"Any weight change to go with it?"* — Appetite and weight together tell a more specific story than either alone — increased appetite with weight loss is a hypermetabolic pattern

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Reduced appetite + weight loss + poor intake** → malignancy, chronic infection, or depression, depending on the accompanying features
- **Increased appetite + weight loss** → hyperthyroidism or uncontrolled diabetes
- **Reduced appetite explained by nausea, pain, or a mechanical cause (dysphagia)** → look for the mechanical/GI explanation first

---

### 5. Lumps and itch — *"Any lumps in the neck, armpits or groin? Any generalised itching?"*

**Why we ask:** Lymphadenopathy is the physical correlate of the lymphoreticular malignancies and chronic infections. Generalised **pruritus without a rash** is a classic paraneoplastic/systemic clue.

**If yes, ask:**
- *"Where are the lumps, and are they painful or painless?"* — Painless, especially in the neck/armpit/groin, raises more concern than tender nodes
- *"How long have they been there, and are they getting bigger?"* — Persistent, enlarging nodes over weeks are more concerning than ones that wax and wane with an infection
- *"Any recent infection nearby, or fever?"* — Tender nodes with a local infection are usually reactive — a benign, self-limiting explanation
- **!** *"Any night sweats or weight loss alongside the lumps?"* — Completes the B-symptom picture and raises lymphoma
- *"Is the itch generalised, and is there any rash?"* — Generalised itch without a visible rash is the classic paraneoplastic/systemic pattern — a rash instead points back to a primary skin cause
- *"Any yellowing of the skin, dark urine, or known kidney or thyroid problems?"* — Screens the major systemic causes of itch without a rash — cholestasis, chronic kidney disease, and thyroid disease

**Rules in / rules out:**
- **Rubbery, painless, persistent nodes ± B symptoms** → lymphoma
- **Tender nodes with local infection** → reactive
- **Generalised itch without rash** → lymphoma (esp. Hodgkin), cholestasis, iron deficiency, chronic kidney disease, polycythaemia, thyroid disease

## Quick-recall list (drill version)

**WASTING:** weight change (quantified, intentional?) · **THERMOSTAT:** fever, rigors, night sweats · **ENERGY:** fatigue, malaise, sleep · **APPETITE:** appetite/intake · **LUMPS:** lumps/lymphadenopathy, generalised itch
